## Shared risks, highest priority first

1. **Too many possible buyers and problems.** Local service businesses are a starting research pool, not a proven market position. All three reviews want a clearer first buyer/problem combination. Narrowing the next test does not commit the business forever to one niche. Property management remains a hypothesis, not the default winner (S03, S05–S06, S16).
2. **A useful method without enough verified commercial evidence.** Existing documents describe sensible sales/delivery procedures, but do not establish repeatable conversion, profitability, quantified outcomes or case-study rights. Do not replace these gaps with guarantees, employer logos, mentor results or simulated buyer enthusiasm (S01, S07, S09–S15, S17).
3. **Solo delivery can become the constraint.** Broad bespoke work, low-priced sites and undefined support can consume capacity before a repeatable business forms. Record discovery, build, revisions, handoff, support and acquisition time rather than judging economics from the quoted fee alone (S08–S12).

## Founder fit and strongest business formulation

Donovin's operations/product background, local familiarity, tool learning and plain-English teaching support a hands-on implementation model. They do not independently establish market fit or technical superiority (S01–S02). The strongest version the board sees is a founder-led service that helps owner-operated businesses resolve a repeated, costly workflow problem: diagnose it, simplify it, implement the smallest credible change, test the result and leave the customer able to operate it. Start with accessible local buyers, develop reusable expertise from accepted work, and broaden only when evidence supports it. This is narrower in execution than a general menu of AI services, while preserving Clockout's existing identity.

The personal educational brand remains valuable in its own right. Teaching employees and everyday learners should not be reduced to lead generation for consulting. Keep a practical buying path for owners and a useful learning path for the broader audience (S16–S18).

## Offer decisions recommended for review

- **Keep central:** the bounded Free Business Look and scoped implementation. Make the first interaction, finding, next choice, deliverable and handoff easy to understand.
- **Keep optional:** deeper paid assessment where a bounded investigation is itself valuable. No mandatory diagnostic fee or universal price is established.
- **Evaluate separately:** the $500 one-page website experiment. Use only when the buyer has a verified presentation/contact problem, with written scope, revision limit, payment milestones, third-party fees and ownership/support boundaries. Measure its own economics. Do not assume it creates demand for retainers.
- **Validate before promoting:** done-with-you/Concierge or managed operations. Recurring billing requires recurring work and reliable monitoring/support. SaaS stays a future possibility.
- **Preserve exclusions:** data brokerage, agency-startup/AI-consulting sales coaching and in-person workshops as Donovin's offer. Older generic playbooks do not override explicit brand boundaries.

These are recommendations, not new active offers. See the complete [offer status and evidence map](evidence-and-questions.md).

## Differences that matter

The votes agree; no dissenting vote is manufactured. The lenses emphasize different standards. Offers is most concerned with buyer confidence, effort and a credible bounded promise. Monopoly is stricter about distribution and durable differentiation; a viable local service need not resemble a venture-scale monopoly. Product prioritizes the first experience, simplicity and consistent handoff. The chair's synthesis is to test a narrow problem and deliver it well, while retaining the broader business identity. It does not require a permanent niche or proprietary software before a first sale.
