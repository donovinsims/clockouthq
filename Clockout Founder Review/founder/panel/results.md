## What would flip a no

- If he sent me that one-pager and it named the exact jobs I lost last month, real numbers off my own missed-call log, and a neighbor I know said he fixed theirs for the $950 without any funny business.
- Two things together: a local reference I can actually call, another trades or dental office owner within an hour of Rockford who will tell me what it did and what it broke, and letting me pay the whole $950 only after I see it run for 30 days on my real missed-call log.
- A name I actually know, a plumbing or HVAC owner within a half hour of me who paid him and can tell me what it did to their callbacks, and I would read the page before he calls me.
- A fixed, small first step I can see the work on before paying anything, like a free one-pager on one of my actual properties plus a flat $250 trial on something blind-spot simple, like missed-call follow-up, with the test written in plain numbers (X calls returned in Y hours) and a reference from a property manager near me who will take my call.
- One of my two owner buddies at the supply house telling me you actually fixed their quotes follow-up and it stuck after 30 days.
- A five-minute call with one other owner in the area, a plumber or another one-truck guy I could actually call, who says the missed-call fix put real booked jobs on his calendar, plus a test written in jobs and dollars, not deliverables, so I can tell if it failed.
- A one-page write-up on my actual business that names something costing me money I had not spotted, plus one client in northern Illinois about my size who will take my call about whether the balance really was not charged.
- An owner I actually know in the trade, someone in my county, with my kind of crew size, telling me Donovin fixed their follow-up or their missed-call log and it stuck. A name and number I can call, not a testimonial page.
- See the written test up front, spelled out in numbers I agree to, before I pay a dollar. If it is specific to missed calls turned into booked jobs, and you show me one local business it worked for, I would probably buy the $950 fix.
- If the free one-pager named a specific dollar figure I am actually losing, quotes going cold, calls sitting in the missed-call log, and it matched what I know is true, then $950 for one narrow fix with the balance held until it passes a written test might be worth a shot.
- Show me a written before-and-after from another landlord or property manager near me, same unit count, the actual dollars and hours, with a phone number I can call.
- If he fixed one thing for free or no money down until the written test passes and it actually worked on a shop I know, I would take him seriously.
