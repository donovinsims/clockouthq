# The competition — Clockout

Research date **2026-11-04**. Reconciliation count: **25 rows — 7 direct providers, 12 indirect providers and 6 substitutes**. The earlier 18-local-provider denominator mixed geographic coverage and cannot be reproduced from the CSV; retain the three named published-price examples without that fraction. Source pages were not re-fetched in this reconciliation. Public sources only; every row in [competitors.csv](competitors.csv) carries the URL it came from. This is a competitor census of what a buyer can actually reach today, not a market-size estimate and not evidence of demand. Prices are copied as published; where a provider publishes nothing, the row says so rather than guessing.

**What is being compared.** Clockout's commercial core is a bounded Free Business Look followed by a scoped, founder-delivered implementation of one workflow (follow-up, scheduling, administration, connected tools), with AI only where it earns its place. The nearest comparable purchase is therefore *a small fixed-scope project that changes how an owner's business runs*, not a SaaS subscription and not an enterprise consulting engagement. Two smaller things are compared separately at the end: the $500 one-page website experiment, and retainers.

## 2. Price range for the comparable item

I compared **a single, fixed-scope project that changes how an owner's business runs**. Scopes are not identical — a website, an AI readiness assessment and a fractional-CFO quarter are different products — so this is a price *band*, not a like-for-like quote. The set used for the median is the published project-level points below; every one is checkable in the CSV.

| Published project-level points | Price |
|---|---:|
| BlueShore.AI readiness assessment | $1,499 |
| TwoCore.ai first build (low) | $1,500 |
| CW Dynamic typical Rockford site (low) | $1,500 |
| Earley Information Science first report | $2,500 |

| Waivium Web Essentials | $3,500 |

| Waivium Web Growth | $6,500 |
| Waivium Web Authority | $15,000 |

- **Lowest published project fee:** $1,499 (national readiness assessment); $1,500 local web project.
- **Median of the ten published points:** **$3,500**.
- **Highest published project fee:** $15,000+ (Waivium Authority).
- **Hourly, for reference:** Upwork median $29.50/hr (marketplace floor) vs. $150–$225/hr middle 50% for fractional operations work (GoFractional, updated 2026-11-04) vs. $25–$199/hr listed on Clutch for AI consulting firms.
- **Retainers, for reference:** $500–$1,500/mo (TwoCore published), $500–$3,500/mo (compiled SMB estimate), **$3,000–$10,000/mo** (fractional CFO in this market), $7,200–$10,800/mo (fractional COO index at ~12 hrs/week). A local retainer is benchmarked against the fractional-ops band whether or not Clockout intends it.
- **Website experiment, compared separately:** the proposed $500 sits far below every published local web price ($1,500 low, $3,500 median, $15,000 high). That is a deliberate cash-and-evidence choice, not a market price, and it should be described as such.

## 5. The gap

**The gap is real in positioning, unproven in demand.** Three things are simultaneously true:

1. **Three named providers serving the area publish a starting price a small owner can act on** (Waivium, CW Dynamic, Corporate Technologies). Everyone else — including both local AI providers — says "request a quote." Complaint #2 says owners resent exactly that.
2. **The one serious local AI capability sells above this buyer.** SparkFire's public logos are a bank, a law firm and Kraft Heinz; its published motion starts with a paid discovery sprint. Stateline AI Solutions is the nearest true rival and already speaks the "practical adoption for local business" language. Nobody local is visibly selling a **$1,500–$3,500 first project to a 5–25-employee owner with the price on the page**.
3. **The national productized offers prove a price point exists**, not that Rockford owners will pay it: Clear Function at $3,500 and Earley at $2,500 publish fixed assessment fees and both credit the fee forward.

**What Clockout can own, if it earns it:** *the operator who shows the work and the price first.* Specifically — a free look that names one bottleneck, a written fixed-scope implementation quoted in the $1,500–$3,500 band (to be set by the CFO work, not by this paragraph), client-owned accounts, one revision round, handover with the customer able to run it, and no retainer obligation to stay live. That combination answers complaints 2, 3, 4 and 5 directly, and it is the one thing no local competitor currently advertises.

**What is *not* a gap.** "AI for small business" is not differentiated — Stateline AI and Illinois AI Consultant already say it. Speed is not differentiated — CW Dynamic advertises 48-hour launches. Local presence is not differentiated — Jumping Trout has 29 years here and Bisconti 30. A $500 website is not differentiated on value — it is below every local price and reads as either a bargain or a warning.

**Honest counter-read.** If a buyer's problem is a bad website, CW Dynamic at $1,500–$5,000 with SEO and care plans is a better-matched purchase than a process consultant, and a $100/mo DIY stack is a rational answer for a two-person shop. The gap is narrow: it exists for owners with a *recurring operational* problem and enough volume to feel it. Finding out whether that owner exists locally is exactly what the consumer panel and real conversations test.

## 6. The threat

**Fastest to copy:** **Stateline AI Solutions** — same geography, same language, veteran-led credibility, already publishing a readiness-audit offer and a free tool. If Clockout publishes a fixed-price first project, they can match it in a week.

**Hardest to beat on capability:** **SparkFire** — real engineering depth, 24/7 operations, brand-name logos, and a published "paid discovery then fixed scope" structure that is the professional version of Clockout's own proposal. They are not chasing micro-businesses, but one referral down-market changes that.

**Commoditizes the offer from below:** a $97/mo GoHighLevel seat plus an Upwork freelancer at $30/hr can imitate "we set up your follow-up" for a fraction of a $2,500 project. Clockout cannot win that comparison on price; it has to win on diagnosis, accountability and the owner not having to manage a freelancer.

**Commoditizes it from above:** Waivium and CW Dynamic already own the local "we will fix your presence" conversation and could bolt on automation messaging without changing their sales motion.

**Structural threats to the business itself:** (a) solo capacity — one 40-hour project and the pipeline goes quiet; (b) no published proof — every local competitor with logos has something Clockout does not yet have; (c) the buyer's default — doing nothing costs nothing today.

---

## What this changes before the next stage

- The comparable project band to test is **$1,500–$3,500**, with $3,500 as the published median of local and national fixed-scope projects. Clockout should not price a first implementation below ~$1,500 without saying plainly that it is buying evidence.
- **Price visibility is the cheapest available differentiator.** Publishing a starting price is a marketing decision that costs nothing and answers the #2 complaint.
- **The $500 website offer is a cash experiment, not a competitive position.** It is 3–7× below the local market; it cannot be the business's public identity.
- **Retainers are benchmarked at $3,000–$10,000/mo locally.** Any future Clockout retainer will be compared to a fractional CFO, so it must carry real operational responsibility, not "monitoring."

Next: **founder-consumer** turns these complaints into the objections the buyer panel tests.
