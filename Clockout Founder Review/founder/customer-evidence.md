## Retrospective interview script — draft, unsent

Donovin conducts these conversations only when authorized. Explain honestly: “I'm reviewing how I scope and deliver projects. I'd like to understand what happened from your side; this isn't a request for a positive review.” Ask one question at a time and follow specifics.

1. What happened that made you seek help at that time? Walk me through the last occurrence.
2. How were you handling it before? What did that cost in time or money, and how do you know?
3. What else did you try or consider? Why did you choose me?
4. What did you expect to receive when you made the first payment?
5. What changed or surprised you once work started? Which delays depended on me, you, or a vendor?
6. What works today? Can we inspect or test it together rather than estimate an impact?
7. What remains unfinished or creates ongoing effort? Who is responsible for it?
8. What would make the next project clearer or easier to approve?
9. If an introduction is appropriate, is there one owner with a similar actual problem you would introduce me to? Record a named next step, not a hypothetical “yes.”

Only after feedback: ask separately whether they would take a reference call or approve a specific factual case summary. Silence or a friendly comment is not permission. Do not ask for a positive testimonial as a condition of support.

## Prospective problem conversation — draft

State that Clockout provides implementation services and is researching a particular workflow; do not disguise selling as neutral research. Ask about the last breakdown, current workaround, actual spend, who owns the decision, urgency, existing platform access and what “working” would mean. Discuss a possible scoped solution only after understanding the problem. End with an appropriate next step: inspect a non-sensitive example, meet the decision maker, or schedule a fit check. An interview, quote, deposit and accepted delivery are different evidence levels.
