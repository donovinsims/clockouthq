# Brand — Clockout

November 4, 2026. **The company keeps its name.** Clockout is Donovin Sims's established business identity, spelled exactly that way, at clockout.us, with the recorded contact clockout815@gmail.com and (608) 713-1651 (reconfirm before external use). This document refines what the name *promises* and how it sounds. It does not rename the company, redraw an existing logo, or claim any trademark clearance.

The brand work below is a proposal informed by simulated personas, not actual acquired buyers. The broad research pool remains owner-led service businesses with roughly 2–25 employees; the 10–25 subgroup and prior-agency experience are unvalidated targeting hypotheses. The two owner-reported projects and their record limitations are in [customer evidence](customer-evidence.md). ## 1. The name, stress-tested (not replaced)

Ten candidates were considered, mostly to test whether "Clockout" is carrying its weight. Kept for reference, not recommended as a rename.

| Candidate | What it says | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Clockout** (current) | End of the shift; done for the day; work finished | Familiar phrase, so hard to search; may read as time-tracking software |
| The One Bottleneck Fix | Exactly what is sold | Long; four words is a mouthful on a sign |
| Clockout One Fix | Brand + product | Slightly awkward rhythm |
| The One-Page Read | Names the free step, not the paid one | Describes the free thing, which is not what earns |
| The Fixed Number | Price transparency | Sounds like an accounting product |
| The Written Test | The anti-change-order artifact | Odd as a product name; strong as a *feature* |
| Bottleneck Audit | Clear category | "Audit" reads as a billable diagnostic — the toll booth buyers resent |
| The Monday Back | Evocative, owner-language | Too clever to say out loud twice |
| Front Office Fix | Plain, trade-adjacent | Generic; six competitors could use it |
| Clockout Front Office | Descriptive combination | Narrows the umbrella identity too early |

**Shortlist for the *offer* (not the company):**
1. **The One Bottleneck Fix** — recommended. Says the job, implies a limit, matches how buyers described their problem.
2. **Clockout One Fix** — if a shorter form is needed on a card or a button.
3. **The One-Page Read** — use as the name of the *free* step only, so the free thing has a name that is not "consultation."

**Company name:** keep **Clockout**. It is already the registered domain, the email, the GitHub org and the identity in Donovin's own content. The name's weakness (it sounds like time-tracking software) is real and is answered by a descriptor, not a rename: *"Clockout — business bottleneck fixes for local service businesses."*

- **Trademark (US):** search [USPTO TESS](https://tmsearch.uspto.gov/) for "CLOCKOUT" as a word mark in **Class 35** (business consulting/advertising services) and **Class 42** (computer/IT services), also checking Class 9 (software) for conflict. A search for "Clockout" plus software/consulting returned nothing usable in this session — that is not clearance. Only a trademark attorney can confirm.
- **Domain:** clockout.us is recorded as already held (verify at the registrar, including expiry and auto-renew). Check whether clockout.com is held and by whom before making any offer on it.
- **Handles:** confirm X, LinkedIn, Instagram, YouTube and TikTok handles match, since personal-brand content links to them. Unverified.
- **Confusion:** no local competitor in [competitors.md](competitors.md) uses a similar name. National check not performed.

Nothing is registered until Donovin registers it. This is not legal advice.

## 2. Voice and promise

**The one-line promise:**
> You will know what is costing you time, what it costs to fix, and what happens if it doesn't work — before you pay anything.

That sentence is the whole brand. It is a promise about *clarity*, not about revenue. It is the one thing five different panel objections all asked for.

**Taglines (three options):**
1. **"Find the bottleneck. Fix the process. Automate the rest."** — the existing line. Keep it as the method statement; it is already Donovin's.
2. **"A fixed number and a written test."** — the sharpest differentiator found in the competitor work.
3. **"Less chasing. More work getting done."** — the most human, best for a homepage headline.

**Voice in three adjectives:**

| Adjective | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| **Plain** | "The quotes nobody follows up." | "Revenue leakage across the intake funnel." |
| **Accountable** | "If it doesn't pass the test, you don't pay the balance." | "Results may vary by business." |
| **Unhurried** | "Sometimes the answer is to use the software you already pay for." | "Transform your business with AI." |

**Before / after, using a line from the pitch:**

- Before: *"I'll look at how your business handles inquiries, quotes and scheduling, find the one step that's costing you the most time, and tell you what I'd fix."*
- After: *"I'll find the step where your quotes go quiet, and tell you what I'd change. One page. No meeting."*

Same offer, 40% shorter, and it names the buyer's actual moment.

## 3. The look, as a brief

Existing visual identity is not audited here and must not be overwritten. If Donovin has a wordmark or colours he likes, they win; the palette below is a direction for the *update*, not a replacement mandate.

**Colour** (contrast computed, WCAG 2.1):

| Role | Hex | On white | Use |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Ink | `#0F172A` | 17.85:1 ✅ AA | Body text, headlines |
| Paper | `#F8FAFC` | — | Page background |
| Slate | `#334155` | 10.35:1 ✅ AA | Secondary text, borders |
| **Accent (amber)** | `#B45309` | 5.02:1 ✅ AA | Primary button, links. On ink background only at 3.56:1 — **large text or icons only**, never small body text |
| Deep navy | `#1E3A5F` | 11.50:1 ✅ AA | Alternative primary if amber feels too warm |
| Confirm | `#15803D` | 5.02:1 ✅ AA | Acceptance/test-passed states |
| Warn | `#B91C1C` | 6.47:1 ✅ AA | Errors, "what didn't pass" |

Two colours carry the brand: **ink** and **one amber accent**. Everything else is a neutral. No gradients, no glow, no "AI purple."

**Type** — both free for commercial use (SIL Open Font License), available from Google Fonts:
- **Display:** Fraunces (or keep whatever the current site uses). Used only for headlines, at large sizes.
- **Text:** Inter. Used for everything else, including the one-page read, the scope sheet and the runbook.

Never a font Donovin has not licensed. No webfont from an unlicensed source.

**Logo brief (only if one does not already exist):**
- Must say: *a shift ending* — work finished, not a time clock being punched.
- Must work at 32px in a browser tab, one colour on a printed page, and as a small mark on a scope sheet.
- Avoid: clock faces with hands (reads as time-tracking SaaS), stopwatch icons, robot or chip imagery, anything that looks like a stock "AI" logo.
- If an image tool is used to explore concepts, those are **concepts**, never a finished logo, and must be checked for similarity against local competitors.

## 4. The first five touchpoints

These are the places a buyer meets the brand, in order. Each has one job.

1. **Homepage (clockout.us, when Donovin updates it):** the promise in the first screen — who it is for, what stops, the fixed number, the free page. Amber primary button, ink text, no carousel.
2. **The One-Page Read (PDF or a single email):** the brand's most important artifact. Ink text on paper white, one page, the owner's own numbers, a plain recommendation, and a signature line with a real phone number.
3. **The Written Test / scope sheet:** the same one-page discipline — what "done" means, in numbers, signed before work starts. This is where trust is actually built.
4. **The Runbook cover:** "How to run this without me" plus how to undo it. The physical proof of the ownership promise.
5. **The reply to the first complaint:** plain, no defensiveness, no corporate voice. *"That's on me. Here's what I'm doing about it, and here's when you'll hear from me again."* This one message sets the brand more than any logo.

## 5. Personal brand relationship

Donovin's teaching identity is not a marketing funnel for Clockout. Two paths, clearly separate:

- **Business path:** Clockout's offer, price, guarantee and contact route.
- **Learning path:** "Learn with Donovin" — links to his public X/LinkedIn, or an education page later. Useful to employees and everyday learners, with no compulsory consulting CTA.

Shared voice, shared name, separate asks. The homepage's main action belongs to the business owner.

## Limits

No trademark search, domain availability check or handle check was performed. No logo was designed or generated. No colours or fonts were applied to anything. The palette and type are recommendations with computed contrast, not an audited existing identity — the current site's actual colours and fonts were not inspected.
