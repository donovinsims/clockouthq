# Clockout website direction — recommendation, not a build spec

November 4, 2026. This is advice for an update Donovin intends to make. **No rebuild, no deployment, no edit to clockout.us happened.** The live site was not inspected in this review; its current content is history, and nothing here should be read as a description of what is on it today.

## Starting point

The site was built months ago and has not been updated. Donovin wants to update it but does not yet know how to present or build it. So this document answers two questions only: **what should it say, and in what order** — plus what to check before anyone builds anything.

## The one job of the homepage

Make one kind of person believe one sentence, and take one small step:

> *"He looks at businesses like mine, tells me the truth for free, and the fix has a fixed price and a written test."*

Everything below serves that. This is not a brand showcase, a service catalogue, or a place to explain AI. The competitor work found that the local market is full of people who look expensive and vague; the simulation categorized trust as the most frequent refusal, alongside habit, price and timing. Real-buyer constraints are unverified. The site's job is proof and plainness.

## Current-pass limitation

The copy below is a historical direction draft. The $950/$2,500 comparisons and public balance-on-acceptance wording are not adopted. Current work prioritizes a scoped, inspected quote before deposit and tests one existing-tool workflow hypothesis; confirm actual terms before turning this draft into public copy. The dated launch calendar has been replaced with relative evidence gates.

## Message hierarchy

**Audience line (first screen, small text above the headline):**
> For owner-led service businesses in the Rockford area.

**Headline — recommended:**
> **Find the bottleneck. Fix the process. Automate the rest.**

Already Donovin's line, already true, and it survives being read by someone who does not know what a workflow is. Two alternatives, in order of preference:

- *"Less chasing. More work getting done."* — warmer, weaker on category.
- *"One bottleneck, one fixed price, one written test."* — sharper for a buyer who already knows what he wants.

**Subhead:**
> Clockout finds the one step where your business loses time — quotes nobody follows up, calls nobody returns, a Monday eaten by paperwork — and fixes it. Sometimes the answer is a process change or the software you already pay for. I'll tell you that instead of selling you something new.

**Method strip (three short blocks, one line each):**
Audit → Optimize → Automate. Under each: what happens, in plain words, not a definition of the word.

**Primary CTA — recommended:**
> **Get the free one-page read**

with one line under it: *"One page on your business: where the time is going, what I'd change, what it's worth. Sent to your phone. No meeting needed."*

Do not use "Book a call" as the primary action. The panel said an hour of their time is the most expensive thing on the page; the read costs them nothing, and its usefulness is a hypothesis to test with real owners.

**Price, visible — this is the differentiator:**
> **$950 for one fix. $2,500 for a whole workflow.** Written down before I start. The balance is due when it passes the test we agreed.

The saved competitor research names three providers serving the area with published starting prices; the former 18-local-provider denominator was not reproducible from the CSV. Price visibility is a hypothesis to test. As [pricing.md](pricing.md) explains, the revised multi-element pitch coincided with a lower simulated PME, but did not isolate price anchoring. Show $950 as the proposed starting rung and $2,500 as the separately scoped Core, subject to owner adoption.

## Page structure

**One page is enough for now.** Six sections, in this order, all above a footer:

1. **Hero** — audience line, headline, subhead, primary CTA, price line.
2. **"Does this sound like you?"** — four recognizable situations, written as the owner's own week: *"The quote goes out and nobody calls it back."* / *"The phone rings while you're under a sink."* / *"Monday is re-typing what you already wrote."* / *"You bought software you barely open."* No invented loss figures.
3. **How it works** — four steps, one line each: the free read → the written test → the fix → the handover and 30-day check. The written test is the section that does the selling; show a blank example of it.
4. **What it costs** — the two prices, what each includes, what is out of scope, the payment split, and the guarantee in one sentence.
5. **Who I am** — a real photograph (with permission), the Roscoe connection, the actual work background. No employer logos, no implied endorsement, no invented credentials. One short paragraph about why he does this, in his voice.
6. **Proof and questions** — see below — then the CTA repeated with the same words as the top.

**Proof section, honestly handled.** There are no verified client results yet. Two acceptable options, in order:

- **Leave the section out** until there is one real result. A missing section is better than a manufactured one.
- **Show a clearly labelled demonstration**: a blank one-page read with synthetic inputs and a note saying so. It shows the mechanism, not a client outcome.

Never: stock testimonials, invented logos, "trusted by", or a competitor's results presented as Donovin's.

**FAQ — the six questions the panel actually asked:**
- *What does "fix" mean?* (Answer with the written test, not a promise.)
- *Do I have to give you my logins?* (No — you create access, you keep ownership, access is revoked at handover.)
- *Will a robot talk to my customers?* (Not without your approval of every word. Draft-only for the first 30 days.)
- *What if it doesn't work?* (You don't pay the balance. Here is what "work" means.)
- *How long does it take?* (State the real estimate and what could move it — no 2-day promise.)
- *Why are you cheaper than an agency?* (Because it's one person, one workflow, no retainer, no account manager. Do not claim to be better — claim to be narrower.)

**Secondary path, small and clearly separate:** a footer link, *"Learn with Donovin"* → his public X/LinkedIn. Employees and everyday learners get real teaching without being pushed into the business funnel. It is a different audience with a different purpose.

**Where the website offer lives:** a single line near the bottom — *"Clockout can also build a clear one-page site for a local business when that's the actual problem; ask if that's what you need."* Link to a separate page or a stated price only if the experiment continues. Never in the hero, never in the price table.

## Calls to action, ranked

| CTA | Where | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Get the free one-page read** | Hero, after pricing, footer | A low-time-cost first step to test; no real conversion evidence |
| **Text or call this number** | Fixed header on mobile | The buyer is in a truck (P001, P006); a form is friction |
| See how it works | Mid-page anchor | For the skeptical reader |
| Learn with Donovin | Footer | Separate audience, separate intent |

One primary action. Three is a menu; a menu is what a buyer with no time ignores.

## Before anyone builds

1. **Check what exists.** Inspect the current site's platform, hosting, domain ownership, analytics and the inquiry route — none audited here. If the inquiry form does not deliver to an inbox Donovin reads, nothing else matters.
2. **Send a test inquiry from a phone** and confirm it arrives, with a timestamp. Then repeat monthly.
3. **Decide the build scope.** The smallest thing that supports this copy: one page, the current stack if it can hold it, no new CRM, no migration, no CMS unless Donovin will actually edit it.
4. **Confirm the claims.** Prices need owner adoption ([pricing.md](pricing.md)); the guarantee needs reviewed wording and the delivered-pilot records in [launch.md](launch.md); references and photographs need permission; confirm the phone number and email.
5. **Check mobile first, keyboard second.** This buyer is on a phone, thumbs only, often outdoors.
6. **Then publish** — after Donovin accepts the copy and all [launch gates](launch.md) pass, including a delivered paid pilot with acceptance and hours recorded. A conversation with one buyer alone does not meet the gate.

Two paid projects are now owner-reported; see [customer evidence](customer-evidence.md). A proof section still requires confirmed delivery facts, measured outcomes for any outcome claim, and explicit publication permission. Payment alone does not grant testimonial rights. Site work remains deferred pending the [launch gates](launch.md).

## What this is not

Not a rebuild instruction, not a platform recommendation, not a design comp, and not evidence that the current site is broken. It is the message and structure this review's evidence supports, for Donovin to accept, edit or reject.
