# Review a local website’s readiness for launch

**Topics** · local-seo, website-conversion, mobile

## When to use it

Use during a lead’s existing-site review or before launching a future local business site. Review the whole site and the inquiry route. Apply the guide to the current brief and use the evidence and access available for that task.

## Inputs

A site URL or preview, confirmed services and coverage, approved proof, the intended inquiry route, and the GBP or confirmed business facts if available.

## Steps

1. Check the homepage and primary service page for a clear offering, real coverage, and a visible next step. Record the page and what a visitor would need to infer.
2. Compare phone, hours, and location or service area across the site and available profile evidence. Ask the owner to resolve discrepancies.
3. On a phone and desktop, check the primary links and form behavior. For an authorized test, use a clearly labeled inquiry and verify both the confirmation and receipt.
4. Classify issues as launch blockers or follow-ups. Treat a broken main inquiry path or materially incorrect business facts as blockers.

## Decision rules

Use an approved alternate contact route when the primary route is blocked and confirm it with the lead. When submission testing is outside scope, report what the interface shows and leave delivery unverified. Numeric performance claims in the post are unverified. A lead review can produce findings and a brief using public or authorized material. Apply changes within the project’s authorized scope.

## Expected output

A readiness checklist with pass, fail, or unknown per item, plus the evidence and owner for each blocker.

## Verification

Retest blockers on the relevant device and page. For forms, distinguish a success message from actual receipt.

## Example

A site shows the right service but the phone link dials an old number. Flag the mismatch as a blocker, confirm the replacement number, and retest the link after an authorized fix.
