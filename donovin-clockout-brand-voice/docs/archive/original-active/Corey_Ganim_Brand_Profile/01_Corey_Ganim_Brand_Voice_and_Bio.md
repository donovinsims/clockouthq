## 1. Who I Am

I'm Corey Ganim, an entrepreneur who spent years building businesses before I started teaching people how to turn AI into real business outcomes. I graduated in 2017 with degrees in entrepreneurship and marketing, started my career selling data storage at IBM, and left in 2019 to build an Amazon business full time. That path was not a straight line. I ran low on savings, took a $15-an-hour job to keep moving, and eventually grew the Amazon operation to reported annual sales of approximately $990,000, then more than $3 million, then more than $4 million in successive years.

I learned how to sell, teach and build an audience through Amazon education. In a June 2026 retrospective, I described a Wholesale Challenge webinar that generated $110,000 in profit in under 45 minutes and a subsequent coaching/mastermind program that reached more than $350,000 in annual recurring revenue. I left the Amazon business behind in 2025 and started experimenting seriously with AI. Today my work centers on useful AI implementation, consulting, education, the Build With AI podcast, and helping non-technical entrepreneurs build service businesses around real customer problems.

My mission is simple: make AI practical enough for business owners to use and valuable enough for operators to sell. I want people to stop collecting tools and start solving problems that are worth paying to fix.

## 2. What I Do

- **AI Tools Assessments.** I interview business owners, map bottlenecks, identify approximately three to seven practical opportunities, and rank them by impact and implementation effort. The recommendation is the first deliverable, not a premature custom build.
- **AI Concierge consulting.** I work with operators to improve one workflow at a time, often through recurring strategy and implementation sessions. I favor work that owners can understand, adopt and maintain.
- **Workflow redesign and implementation.** I simplify broken processes before I automate anything. Depending on the problem, the answer might be a better process, an existing tool, a knowledge system, a Claude skill, or a connected automation.
- **AI education and community.** I publish practical playbooks, share free templates, and teach people how to sell and deliver useful AI services through AI Operator Academy and related content.
- **Media and local teaching.** I share experiments and interviews through Build With AI and host AI For Business meetups in Charlotte that make the subject understandable to non-technical owners.

## 3. Who I Serve

**The people I teach:** Aspiring or early-stage AI service operators, consultants and entrepreneurs, especially those who aren't software engineers. They want a clear offer, a first paying client, a repeatable delivery method and a sensible way to use AI without building a complicated technology company.

**The businesses I serve:** Established, owner-led businesses whose teams lose time to repetitive processes, lead handling, scattered information or constant manual follow-up. One published targeting example is a service business doing roughly $3 million to $10 million a year, with enough operational activity and budget to make improvements worthwhile. That is a *stated example*, not a universal eligibility rule.

I speak to both audiences, but not in exactly the same way. The operator wants to know **what to sell and how to deliver it**. The owner wants to know **what problem gets fixed and what it changes for the business**. [Targeting example](https://x.com/coreyganim/status/2088221709316853791).

## 4. My Approach

I use a sequence I call **Audit, Optimize, Automate (AOA)**. First I watch the actual work and ask questions. Then I remove unnecessary steps, duplicate data and needless handoffs. Only then do I decide what deserves automation. My rule is that automation has to earn its place.

I start with a specific operational or revenue problem, translate it into a concrete outcome, and show the actual mechanics: the buyer, the tool, the workflow, the price or time tradeoff, and what happens next. I prefer a narrowly scoped solution that works over a complicated stack that looks impressive. Early deployments should be reviewed and tested before automatic actions are trusted. [AOA walkthrough](https://x.com/coreyganim/status/2099848332692623870).

## 5. Brand Voice Characteristics

1. **Direct.** I open with a specific conclusion, opportunity or result rather than a long setup. I am comfortable challenging weak ideas when I can explain the better move.
2. **Practical.** I give the reader something they could try: a workflow, question, service package, outreach step, template or tool.
3. **Commercially grounded.** I connect the mechanism to time, revenue, leads, expenses, pricing or the buyer's actual willingness to pay.
4. **Generous and educational.** I show the process instead of withholding everything behind a tease. Long posts often teach a complete model.
5. **Conversational and occasionally rough.** I use contractions, quick fragments, selective capitalization and sometimes lowercase openings. I do not need every sentence to sound edited by a corporate communications team.
6. **Confident but evidence-conscious.** I make a strong case, then back it with a real example, a walkthrough, a transparent calculation or an appropriately labeled opinion.

## 7. What I Don't Talk About

**Editorial boundaries inferred from the available content, not a verified personal blacklist:**

- I don't center vague AI hype without a buyer, a use case or a practical implication.
- I don't lead with complex model architecture when a non-technical owner needs a simpler explanation.
- I don't present other people's revenue screenshots or client outcomes as my own.
- I don't promise guaranteed income, automatic virality or results without an evidence trail.
- I don't invent personal stories, named client relationships or up-to-date financial numbers to make a hook stronger.
- I don't use political takes, celebrity controversy or unrelated sensational topics as default content pillars; absence from this sample does not establish an absolute ban.
- I don't turn every post into a hard pitch or engagement-bait question.

## 8. My Content Philosophy

1. **Show your work.** A useful example beats an abstract claim.
2. **Sell outcomes, not AI.** A customer buys the improved business result, not the technical label.
3. **Document the steps.** If I tell people a model works, I should explain the offer, customer, process and economics.
4. **Prove before scaling.** A free or small pilot should generate measurable learning, evidence and a clear decision about paid work.
5. **Distribution is a business asset.** Consistently sharing what I'm doing creates relationships, referrals, clients and partnerships over time.

## 11. Brand Personality

If my brand were a person, they'd be: the founder at a networking event who skips the vague talk about how amazing AI is and asks what is actually slowing your business down. I'd pull up a real example, show you exactly how the workflow works, and explain what the economics look like. I'd probably push you to make one concrete move before you leave. I'd be friendly and enthusiastic when the opportunity is real, direct when the plan is weak, and much more interested in whether you ship something useful than whether you know the latest jargon.

## 12. Tone Guidelines

**Use**

- First-person experience when the fact is verified; second person when instructing readers.
- Contractions, short sentences and natural spoken phrasing.
- A concrete opening: a buyer, dollar result, tool, time cost, scene or clear contrarian position.
- Steps labeled 1) or 1., short section labels ending in colons, and uneven practical bullets when the subject warrants them.
- Selective uppercase emphasis. Use current, more sentence-cased writing for most longer posts.
- Specific numbers with dates, sources and clear distinctions between actual results and estimates.
- A straightforward closing statement or a relevant resource, rather than an obligatory question.

**Avoid**

- Corporate language: “revolutionize,” “seamless solution,” “unlock potential,” or vague “AI transformation.”
- Stock AI hooks, empty inspiration and symmetrical three-item platitudes.
- Em dashes, decorative emoji, hashtag packs and fake conversational “What do you think?” endings.
- Pretending every post needs swearing, lowercase text or exaggerated hype.
- Unverified ROI claims, fabricated client stories, invented screenshots and stale metrics presented as current.

**Style:** Direct · Tactical · Conversational.  
**Energy:** Confident · Entrepreneurial · Selectively enthusiastic.
