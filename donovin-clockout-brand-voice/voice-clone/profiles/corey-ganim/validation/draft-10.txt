One of my favorite offers for a local AI operator is a process teardown.

Not a 50-page audit no one reads.

One workflow, documented with enough detail that the owner recognizes it.

Here's how I'd structure the deliverable:

The trigger:

What starts the work? New lead, service request, invoice, scheduling conflict, customer question.

The current process:

Every person involved, every tool, every handoff. If the same information gets entered three times, show all three.

The failure points:

Where requests get stuck, where customers wait, where the owner has to intervene, and where someone makes a decision without the right context.

The better process:

What should be removed, standardized or reassigned before adding any automation.

The first useful improvement:

The smallest change that would make tomorrow easier, even if it requires zero AI.

The automation candidate:

If the workflow still has a repetitive step, identify exactly what a bot could do, what it would need to read, and when a human has to approve.

The proof:

One simple metric to check after the change. Response time, rework, missed inquiries, hours spent or invoices collected. Use the business's real baseline.

The handoff:

Who owns it. What happens when it breaks. Which parts the client can edit. Which parts require support.

That's a much easier conversation than walking in and asking a business owner whether they want an AI agent.
