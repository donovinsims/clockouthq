The simplest way to sell managed AI agents if you have zero case studies:
A 30-day paid pilot with one job.
The full structure in 9 steps (#7 is the one page that earns the renewal):

## Post 2 of 12

https://x.com/coreyganim/status/2100257667348656331

1.
Give the agent one job
Not "AI employee." One defined function.
Speed to lead. Speed to quote. Lead qualification.
If you can't say what it does in five words, the owner can't either, and they won't pay for what they can't describe.

## Post 3 of 12

https://x.com/coreyganim/status/2100257679050715493

2.
Week 1: pick the job
Write down three things.
What information comes in. What access the agent needs. How long the work takes a person today.
That last number is your ROI line for the entire pilot.

## Post 4 of 12

https://x.com/coreyganim/status/2100257690857714096

3.
Week 2: build two tasks in shadow mode
Pick two tasks the client repeats often.
Shadow mode means real work, but the agent can't send a message or change a record on its own.
The owner sees what it would have done before it's allowed to do anything.

## Post 5 of 12

https://x.com/coreyganim/status/2100257702543024638

4.
Week 3: one shared channel
The client, the agent, and you in the same place.
Watch every request. Save the important results somewhere everyone can read.
This is where the owner stops thinking of it as software and starts thinking of it as a team member.

## Post 6 of 12

https://x.com/coreyganim/status/2100257714366788038

5.
Week 4: fix what broke
Check the scorecard with the client.
Decide together whether a second task is worth adding.
Something will have failed. Fixing it in front of them is worth more than a pilot where nothing went wrong.

## Post 7 of 12

https://x.com/coreyganim/status/2100257726014292007

6.
The end-of-month meeting has five questions
-What work was completed?
-How much human checking was still needed?
-What broke?
-What value did the client confirm?
-What task should come next?
Answer all five and the renewal conversation is already over.

## Post 8 of 12

https://x.com/coreyganim/status/2100257737636720696

7.
The Friday scorecard
One page, every week:
-work completed
-time the work used to take
-time the AI took
-errors or exceptions
-human approvals requested
-next workflow to test
A friend's client scorecard showed 63 hours and $6,300 of value in one week. Estimates, but the client can see the math on paper.

## Post 9 of 12

https://x.com/coreyganim/status/2100257749322141915

8.
Price it like a pilot, then raise it
A friend who runs managed agents started at $500 setups.
$500 became $1,000. He's now targeting $2,000 setups plus $1,000 a month per agent.
Every yes moves the next number. The pilot price is the floor, not the offer.

9.
The pilot is the sales process
Getting the agent running earns attention.
Keeping it reliable, helping the client use it, and proving the value every week is what gives them a reason to keep paying.
