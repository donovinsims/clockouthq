The stack

The bot has one job: get me quoted and featured in traditional media by running the real Newsjack skills.

Four pieces make that work:

A dedicated Grok Bot. Its only job is PR. It runs the routines, applies the rules, and messages me when something is worth sending.

Newsjack. The skills (PR judgment) plus a small command-line engine (news ingestion, dedupe, freshness checks). Free, auto-updates.

Medialyst. News search plus a free journalist-request feed that covers HARO, Connectively, MentionMatch, LinkedIn, X, and Substack. Paid list jobs exist, but mine only run with my approval.

A ledger. One CSV row per draft: outlet, reporter, story, editor score, status, reply, placement. I mirror it into Notion as a tracker.

Keep it a separate bot. A PR bot with one job and its own rules is far easier to trust than a general assistant that also does PR.

Phase 2: Feed it real inputs

I build every system the same way: Audit, Optimize, Automate (AOA). For a PR bot, that means getting the inputs right before anything runs on a schedule.

Have the bot run these in order:

/pr-strategist. Audience, positioning, news pegs. Mine landed on one play: be the practical source on AI agents for small businesses, built on "fix the process before you automate it." Cost: $1.05.

/voice-extractor. I fed it 20 LinkedIn posts I wrote by hand (3,616 words). It fingerprints sentence length, word choice, and punctuation, then enforces that voice on every draft. Cost: $1.50.

/newsjack-monitor-setup. Topics, competitors, feeds. Mine has 22 search terms and 3 feeds.

A proof-points file. Only claims I'll defend publicly, each with a source.

20 target outlets across 3 tiers, reporter names left blank until a story needs one.

Save profile.json flat. Top-level keys only, never wrapped inside a "profile" key. The detector ignores a wrapped profile without warning. In testing, the same content wrapped produced 0 search queries. Flat, it produced 18 queries and 113 scored signals.

Phase 3: Schedule the routines

Now the Automate step. Set these four as recurring routines on your Grok Bot:

 

The morning slot gets drafts ready before noon, when editors read most. The midday sweep exists because request deadlines are often hours away. Every 90 days, the bot refreshes my voice fingerprint.

Skip Newsjack's own scheduler. The bot's routines handle timing, so there's one schedule to manage, not two.

A pitch pack is six skills in a row: /headline-generator, a 51 to 150 word pitch in my voice, /fact-check, /meanest-editor, /find-journalists, and /same-outlet-ranker to pick one reporter per outlet.

Phase 4: The Three-Gate Rule

Every draft has to clear The Three-Gate Rule before it reaches me:

The Editor Gate. /meanest-editor roasts the pitch like a veteran PR director. It gets up to 2 revisions. Under 7/10, I never see it.

The Fact Gate. /fact-check verifies every claim. Anything unverified goes under "Needs from you," not into the pitch.

The Human Gate. The bot never emails, DMs, submits, or creates a mailbox draft. I send everything.

Hard caps on top: 3 pitches a day, 5 reactive replies a week, one reporter per outlet per story.

If nothing clears the gates, I get no message. Silence is a feature.

One platform rule: Qwoted bans AI-written answers. My bot flags Qwoted requests, and I write those myself.

The "Needs from you" list is where I earn the placement. Reporter emails stay blank because /find-journalists runs in local mode and never guesses contacts. I look them up, or approve a paid Medialyst list job for that exact story.

Send-by times are real. On day one, one pitch had a noon deadline and the other 3 PM.

Journalist requests work the same way, with three verdicts: Respond, Ask for proof, or Kill. On a Respond, I rewrite the draft in my own words. My first one went to a CEO-focused publication. The bot drafted 77 words. I added the 3 workflows I showed 45 realtors at a workshop. Only I could add that.

Two more habits:

Follow-ups: one, after 3 to 7 days, only if I ask.

Fridays: I read the scorecard (drafted, sent, replied, placed, repeat reporters) and approve up to 3 brief.md tweaks. At weeks 4 and 12, if reply rate is under 3%, I tighten fit before adding volume.
