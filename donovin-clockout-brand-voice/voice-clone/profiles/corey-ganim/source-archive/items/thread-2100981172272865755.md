1.
Find the gap
Charlotte already has a big monthly AI meetup for developers.
There was nothing for non-technical business owners, and there are way more of them than devs.
Whatever city you're in, that gap almost certainly exists.

2.

Don't invent. Copy.

3.
Book the room
A library is free. A co-working space runs about $200 for 2 hours.
Have Claude rank every nearby co-working space by capacity, AV, and parking, then booked the top pick on the spot.
~$120 for two hours.

4.
The 90-minute format
20 minutes of networking.
One simple live AI demo.
More networking.
A soft CTA at the very end. No pitch deck, no pressure.
Network for 60 minutes. Teach for 30. Sell for 30 seconds.

6.
The one registration question
The Luma form takes under 60 seconds and asks one money question:
"What's the number one thing you want AI to fix?"
That answer tells you who's hot before they walk in the door.

## Post 8 of 13

https://x.com/coreyganim/status/2100981255076769808

7.
The 30-second pitch
"If any of you want a completely free AI assessment, where we sit down for 15 minutes and uncover one bottleneck in your business where AI can help, I'm happy to do that free of charge. I'll be following up with each of you individually. See you next month."
That's it. Short, non-salesy, and it feeds every paid offer.

## Post 9 of 13

https://x.com/coreyganim/status/2100981267047293315

8.
The follow-up rule
"I'll be following up with each of you individually" is the most important sentence in the pitch.
Now the text and email they get within 24 hours is expected, not cold.
The follow-up closes the deals. Not the stage.

9.
Define success before event one
What criteria will you use to determine success?
Is it number of paid clients? Number of attendees? Dollars attributed?
Figure this out from day one, then track it.
