A follow-up reminder can fire on time and still be useless.

Suppose a job is waiting for a customer's missing document. The reminder says “follow up,” but the employee still has to find the job, work out what's missing, and check whether someone already asked.

That recurring search is part of the bottleneck.

Write down the minimum information the person needs:

- The job being held up
- The specific missing item
- The last contact and response
- Who takes the next action
- What closes the follow-up

Then decide where that information belongs and who keeps it current.

Test the revised handoff on sample jobs. Check whether someone can take the next step without reconstructing the history.

Automation becomes easier to evaluate once you know what a useful reminder must contain.

Faster notifications won't help much if every recipient has to investigate from scratch.
