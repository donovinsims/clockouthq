## PURPOSE AND OPERATING STANDARD

This document establishes a repeatable system for researching, designing, developing, launching, and optimizing exceptional websites for established local service businesses.

It covers two connected areas:

1. Website strategy and execution: Comprehensive strategy covering website design, conversion optimization, local SEO, customer trust, engineering, analytics, and a nine-phase AI development workflow.
2. Regional execution: Regional prospect intelligence, competitive positioning, affordable website delivery, local business qualification, community research, and practical sales-to-launch execution.

The guidance has been verified against current Google, W3C, web.dev, and FTC documentation as of the research cutoff above.

Third-party sources, competitor marketing, and industry statistics are reference inputs, not unquestionable authorities.

### Primary objective

Every Clockout website must help the client achieve some combination of these outcomes:

- Generate more qualified phone calls, quote requests, bookings, and customer inquiries.
- Communicate services, service areas, and value immediately.
- Establish credibility and distinguish the business from competitors.
- Improve the business’s technical foundation for local organic search.
- Strengthen the customer experience connecting Google Business Profile to the website.
- Reduce friction between customer discovery and contacting the business.
- Present an exceptional mobile-first visual experience.
- Remain fast, secure, accessible, maintainable, and affordable.
- Support future expansion when actual business demand justifies it.

Ultimate measure of success: qualified inquiries and business outcomes, not website appearance or SEO scores alone.

### Evidence classification

All research and recommendations must be assigned one of four classifications:

- VERIFIED STANDARD: Supported by authoritative documentation, established specifications, or directly observed facts.
- RESEARCH-SUPPORTED PRACTICE: Supported by credible UX research or reproducible evidence, but results may differ across businesses.
- PROFESSIONAL JUDGMENT: A reasonable design, technical, commercial, or strategic recommendation that has not been proven universally.
- UNVERIFIED CLAIM: A source assertion, competitor marketing claim, anecdote, statistic, or inference requiring additional validation.

AI agents must never turn an unverified claim into a verified fact.

# PART A: EXECUTIVE STRATEGY

## A1. The seven principles of exceptional local business websites

### 1. Customer intent determines the design

A website is not primarily an online brochure. It is an interface between a customer with a problem and a business capable of solving it.

Before choosing page layouts, typography, components, or imagery, determine:

- Why is the customer searching?
- What problem needs solving?
- Is the situation urgent?
- What information would make them comfortable contacting the business?
- What is their preferred next action?
- What might stop them from taking that action?

**Examples:**

#### Emergency plumber

- Customer wants immediate assistance.
- Availability and response options are critical.
- Primary CTA should prioritize calling.
- Avoid lengthy forms and distracting navigation.

#### Auto repair shop

- Customer needs to understand the services offered.
- Location, business hours, and scheduling matter.
- Primary CTA may be calling or booking an appointment.

#### Roofing contractor

- Customer may be comparing multiple estimates.
- Project examples, credentials, insurance, and process transparency matter.
- Primary CTA is often a quote or inspection request.

#### Residential property manager

- Property owners want reduced workload, reliable maintenance, better tenant management, and financial visibility.
- Tenants want maintenance help, rent payment, and clear communication.
- These are distinct audiences requiring separate navigation and conversion paths.

Rule: Design around real customer behavior and business operations, not generic agency templates.

### 2. Immediate clarity takes priority over visual decoration

The initial viewport should quickly communicate:

- What the company does.
- Who it serves.
- Where it operates.
- Why customers should trust it.
- What visitors should do next.

The familiar three-second or five-second test is a useful usability heuristic, not a universal scientific threshold.

A visitor should not need to decode abstract slogans.

**Weak headline:**

Excellence in Every Solution.

**Stronger headline:**

Furnace Repair & Installation in Rockford, Illinois.

Stronger when the differentiator is verified:

Rockford Furnace Repair With Clear Estimates Before Work Begins.

Use clear supporting text, a primary CTA, and authentic proof.

### 3. Trust must be demonstrated

Trust is strongest when supported by observable evidence.

**Prioritize:**

- Real customer reviews.
- Verified license and certification information.
- Authentic project photography.
- Owner and team information.
- Legitimate operating history.
- Clear business hours.
- Transparent service areas.
- Realistic warranties and guarantees.
- Accurate contact information.

Do not fabricate proof or exaggerate the client’s capabilities.

### 4. Mobile is the default design environment

Design the primary customer journey on a narrow mobile screen first.

Use desktop space to improve presentation, not to create a completely different purchasing experience.

**Priorities:**

- Readable headlines.
- Visible contact actions.
- Accessible navigation.
- Short forms.
- Clear trust signals.
- Fast initial rendering.
- Unobstructed content.
- Easy thumb interaction.

Never treat desktop screenshots as sufficient proof of quality.

### 5. Search visibility starts with relevance and usefulness

Google Search and Google Maps use related but different systems.

A website should communicate accurate services, customer needs, expertise, and geography.

Google describes its primary local ranking considerations as relevance, distance, and prominence/popularity.

**Consequently:**

- A website cannot eliminate geographic proximity limitations.
- Adding city keywords does not guarantee Maps rankings in those cities.
- A well-optimized site does not guarantee top organic rankings.
- Google Business Profile optimization does not replace website SEO.
- Schema markup is not a substitute for useful content.

### 6. Every additional feature needs a reason

Avoid shipping features because they are fashionable.

Examples of unnecessary complexity:

- AI chatbots without proven demand.
- Multiple appointment platforms.
- Heavy animations.
- Complicated navigation.
- Unused CRM integrations.
- Excessive JavaScript.
- Generic blog sections with no content strategy.
- Dozens of nearly identical city pages.

The best implementation is the simplest one that achieves the intended customer and business outcome.

### 7. Launch is the beginning of measurement

A successful site launch establishes:

- Working contact mechanisms.
- Accurate business information.
- Accessible, responsive design.
- Crawlable public pages.
- Reliable hosting.
- A clear path to measuring inquiries.

Organic traffic, rankings, customer acquisition, and profitability must be evaluated afterward.

Do not promise that launching a new site will immediately improve rankings or lead volume.

## A2. Clockout’s recommended website delivery model

Do not force every client into the same package.

Use three scope levels.

### Level 1: Essential Local Website

Recommended default for affordable one-time website projects.

**Typical scope:**

- One custom, responsive homepage.
- Clearly organized service sections.
- Primary service area.
- Verified trust signals.
- Authentic photography where available.
- Click-to-call and contact flow.
- Contact form with tested delivery.
- Basic on-page SEO.
- Appropriate business structured data.
- HTTPS and basic technical QA.
- Client-controlled website assets and documented handoff.

**Best for:**

- Businesses without a real website.
- Facebook-only businesses.
- Small service providers with a focused offering.
- Businesses needing immediate credibility and reliable contact access.

**Important:**

An excellent one-page website is preferable to a poorly researched 10-page website.

### Level 2: Local Growth Website

**Typical scope:**

- Homepage.
- Two to five dedicated service or audience pages.
- About or credentials page.
- Contact or booking page.
- Relevant internal links.
- Deeper SEO targeting.
- More comprehensive project examples.
- Search Console and conversion tracking, if included in the agreement.

**Best for:**

- Businesses offering multiple materially different services.
- Firms with distinct customer types.
- Businesses with meaningful search demand across service categories.
- Established companies seeking stronger organic search coverage.

### Level 3: Competitive Local Search Website

**Typical scope:**

- Intent-driven service pages.
- Genuinely distinct geographic pages where justified.
- Project or case-study library.
- Industry expertise content.
- Advanced analytics.
- Ongoing GBP management.
- Citation and reputation work.
- Ongoing SEO measurement and optimization.

**Best for:**

- Established businesses in highly competitive markets.
- Companies with strong customer acquisition economics.
- Businesses willing to invest in ongoing improvements.
- Multi-location or multi-service organizations.

Commercial guardrail: Treat a roughly \$500 one-time website price as an example offer configuration, not a universal required price. At approximately that price, a carefully scoped one-page build is generally a more defensible default than promising four custom pages, extensive SEO, GBP management, analytics, and ongoing support.

Domain fees, paid integrations, content production, ongoing maintenance, and expanded SEO must be disclosed and scoped separately.

## A3. The biggest expensive mistakes

1. Designing before understanding the business.
2. Rebuilding an existing website without preserving valuable URLs and SEO equity.
3. Publishing inaccurate or invented business claims.
4. Using generic AI copy and stock photography as the primary brand identity.
5. Building dozens of unoriginal location pages.
6. Investing in animations instead of fixing customer contact friction.
7. Treating a passing Lighthouse score as proof of conversion performance.
8. Shipping an untested contact form.
9. Using SEO promises to sell an inexpensive website build.
10. Making the client dependent on the builder’s personal accounts.
11. Adding recurring software costs without explaining them.
12. Designing for the business owner’s preferences while ignoring customer needs.
13. Ignoring what happens after the customer submits an inquiry.
14. Assuming reviews, backlinks, schema, or GBP posts guarantee rankings.

## B1. Local market research and competitor intelligence

### Target geography

**Primary:**

- Rockford
- Roscoe
- Rockton
- Loves Park
- Machesney Park
- South Beloit
- Winnebago
- Cherry Valley
- Pecatonica
- Durand
- Other relevant Winnebago County communities

**Secondary:**

- Belvidere and Boone County.
- Beloit, Wisconsin.
- Nearby Southern Wisconsin communities.
- Relevant Northern Illinois service markets.

Do not claim a business serves every location merely because Clockout targets that region.

### Required research before design

**Collect:**

#### Business facts

- Registered and public-facing business name.
- Business type.
- Core services.
- Most valuable services.
- Service boundaries.
- Customer types.
- Physical storefront or service-area business status.
- Contact details and hours.
- Verified licensing and professional credentials.

#### Customer intelligence

- Common reasons customers contact the business.
- Urgent versus planned service needs.
- Frequently asked questions.
- Common objections.
- Preferred contact channels.
- Typical inquiry-to-sale process.
- Problems the business is especially qualified to solve.

#### Competitive intelligence

- Search competitors.
- Direct business competitors.
- Google Maps competitors.
- Industry positioning.
- Service differentiation.
- Visible credibility signals.
- Website conversion approaches.
- Content gaps.
- Customer language from reviews.

### Distinguish three competitor categories

**Direct competitors**  
Businesses serving the same customers with similar services.

**Organic search competitors**  
Websites appearing for the target service queries, including directories and national platforms.

**Maps competitors**  
Businesses repeatedly appearing in local Maps results for relevant searches.

These groups overlap but are not identical.

### Repeatable search process

**For each business:**

1. Identify five to ten relevant service queries.
2. Include primary city combinations.
3. Include specific service problems.
4. Include emergency or appointment modifiers when relevant.
5. Search across the relevant geographic area.
6. Review desktop and mobile results.
7. Record recurring organic and Maps competitors separately.
8. Select four to six competitors for deeper inspection.

**Example queries:**

- plumber Rockford IL
- sump pump replacement Roscoe
- water heater repair Loves Park
- HVAC repair Machesney Park
- roof replacement Rockton
- wheel alignment Loves Park
- property management Rockford IL
- rental property management Roscoe

Search results must be recorded with date, approximate search location, and device assumptions.

Incognito mode alone does not reliably reproduce another customer’s precise local search results.

### Competitor audit fields

**Capture:**

- Business name and URL.
- Date inspected.
- Primary service and location.
- Search result or discovery source.
- First-screen headline.
- Primary CTA.
- Mobile phone access.
- Form or booking path.
- Service detail quality.
- Relevant locations and geographic specificity.
- Real imagery versus generic imagery.
- Review and credential evidence.
- Performance findings, if actually tested.
- Visible accessibility or usability issues.
- Content gaps.
- Positioning differences.
- One concrete improvement opportunity.

For defects, preserve a screenshot or direct evidence.

Do not label a website slow without a measured test.

Do not label a business invisible in Google without documenting the actual query and search context.

### Suggested competitor scoring

Use a diagnostic score, not an invented ranking score.

Evaluate each category on a 0-to-2 scale:

- Service clarity.
- Location clarity.
- Primary CTA.
- Mobile contact accessibility.
- Authentic trust signals.
- Service content.
- Customer objection handling.
- Differentiation.
- Navigation.
- Lead-capture functionality.
- Visual professionalism.
- Technical usability.

**Interpretation:**

- 0 = Missing or demonstrably ineffective.
- 1 = Present but weak or incomplete.
- 2 = Clear and effective based on observed evidence.
- N/A = Not relevant or not verifiable.

Keep the score separate from actual organic ranking, Maps ranking, or business revenue.

## B2. Finding high-value Clockout website prospects

Prospecting across Winnebago County small businesses tends to surface recurring patterns, which are reflected in the diagnostic below.

Treat these patterns as hypotheses. Treat any individual business finding as a lead requiring fresh inspection before it is used in outreach.

Agents may research prospects and draft outreach or proposals, but may not contact prospects, send messages, or make commitments without Donovin’s explicit approval.

### The 60-second diagnostic

Look for at least one concrete business-impacting issue.

#### Priority 1: No usable website

- No website linked from Google Business Profile.
- Only a social media page.
- Domain no longer resolves.
- Main site unavailable.

#### Priority 2: Broken contact conversion

- Phone number cannot be tapped on mobile.
- Contact form visibly fails.
- Booking link is broken.
- Contact information is difficult to find.

#### Priority 3: Broken mobile experience

- Horizontal scrolling.
- Unreadable text.
- Navigation obstructs content.
- Critical interaction does not work.

#### Priority 4: Security or technical failure

- HTTPS unavailable.
- Certificate errors.
- Important pages return errors.
- Public site accidentally blocked from indexing.

#### Priority 5: Major clarity or credibility gap

- Core services are unclear.
- No obvious next action.
- No business identity or real proof.
- Important information is outdated.

### Prospect qualification

**Prioritize businesses with:**

- Genuine local operations.
- An identifiable decision-maker.
- Evidence of active customers.
- A visible website problem.
- Services with meaningful financial value.
- A realistic reason to invest in an improvement.

**Useful supporting signals:**

- Recent legitimate customer reviews.
- Active business operations.
- Existing investment in a domain or advertising.
- Real project photography.
- Relevant business growth activity.

Avoid assuming review count proves revenue or purchasing intent.

### Commercial value test

Estimate whether the website investment could plausibly be worthwhile.

**Use:**

`Estimated jobs required to recover website cost = website cost / contribution profit per incremental job`

**Example:**

- Website build: \$600.
- Estimated contribution profit per additional job: \$300.
- Two incremental jobs could recover the build cost.

This is an illustrative calculation, not a sales guarantee.

Do not confuse a job’s total invoice amount with profit.

### When to skip

Do not prospect solely because a website looks old.

**If a business already has:**

- Clear service information.
- Reliable contact functionality.
- Good mobile usability.
- Strong trust signals.
- A maintainable website.

Then only approach if a material, demonstrable opportunity exists.

## B3. Website information architecture

### One-page versus multi-page decision

**Choose one page when:**

- The business has a focused set of services.
- Most visitors have the same conversion goal.
- Content fits naturally into organized sections.
- Budget is limited.
- There is insufficient evidence to justify separate pages.

**Choose multiple pages when:**

- Services have distinct search intent.
- Customers need substantial service-specific explanations.
- The company serves different audiences.
- There is enough verified content to support useful individual pages.
- Search demand and commercial value justify the added work.

### Recommended one-page architecture

1. Header and navigation.
2. Hero and primary CTA.
3. Trust and credibility proof.
4. Services.
5. Why choose the business.
6. Real work or project examples.
7. How the service process works.
8. Testimonials.
9. Service area.
10. FAQs.
11. Final CTA and contact form.
12. Footer.

These are building blocks, not a mandatory identical layout for every client.

### Recommended multi-page architecture

#### Homepage

- Primary service category.
- Main location or service region.
- Overview of important services.
- Trust.
- Primary conversion action.

#### Service pages

- One genuine customer need or meaningful service category.
- Detailed answers.
- Related projects.
- Service-specific CTA.
- Relevant internal links.

#### About page

- Authentic company story.
- Team.
- Qualifications.
- Differentiation.
- Service standards.

#### Contact page

- Phone.
- Form.
- Hours.
- Public business location when applicable.
- Service area.
- Directions if customers visit the business.

#### Additional content

- Case studies.
- Genuine location-specific pages.
- Helpful FAQs and resources.

### Page expansion rule

A page should only be created when it serves a distinct, useful customer purpose.

Before adding a page, the agent must answer:

1. Which customer search or decision does this page serve?
2. What information is genuinely different from existing pages?
3. What verified evidence supports the content?
4. How will visitors reach the page?
5. What conversion action should the page produce?

If these cannot be answered, do not create the page.

## B4. Premium visual design system

### General design direction

Clockout websites should feel:

- Established.
- Trustworthy.
- Polished.
- Contemporary.
- Locally relevant.
- Easy to understand.
- Distinctive to the specific business.

**Avoid two common mistakes:**

#### Generic AI design

- Repetitive cards.
- Oversized gradients.
- Generic stock portraits.
- Excessive iconography.
- Artificial-looking testimonials.
- Identical hero layouts across different industries.

#### Overdesigned agency portfolios

- Excessive animation.
- Minimal readability.
- Abstract headlines.
- Decorative interfaces hiding practical information.
- Complex navigation without customer benefit.

### Typography

**Recommendations:**

- Choose one strong primary typeface.
- Optionally pair it with one complementary display font.
- Use a consistent type scale.
- Maintain readable line lengths.
- Prefer 16px or greater mobile body text as a practical design default.
- Use comfortable line spacing.
- Avoid low-contrast, extremely light font weights.
- Ensure text remains legible at 200% zoom.

Typography should reflect the business.

**Examples:**

- HVAC and electrical: confident, practical, clear.
- Roofing: strong, established, trustworthy.
- Residential property management: structured, calm, professional.
- Salon: distinctive, refined, editorial.
- Auto repair: straightforward, technical, credible.

### Color

Create a small token-based palette:

- Primary brand color.
- Primary foreground color.
- Main background.
- Secondary surface.
- Accent or CTA color.
- Border color.
- Error and success states.

Do not choose colors solely because they appear popular in AI-generated designs.

Start with the client’s actual visual identity.

### Layout

**Use:**

- Consistent content widths.
- Clear visual hierarchy.
- Predictable spacing.
- Responsive grids.
- Deliberate section transitions.
- Balanced image-to-text relationships.
- Reusable components.

Suggested development review widths:

- 360px.
- 390px.
- 768px.
- 1024px.
- 1440px.

These are test points, not the only supported screen widths.

Use fluid layouts rather than hardcoding designs to these exact viewport sizes.

### Photography

**Preferred asset order:**

1. Professionally captured client-owned photos.
2. Good-quality real photos from the client.
3. Existing authorized business imagery.
4. Carefully selected licensed supplementary photography.
5. Illustration where it genuinely clarifies information.

**Prioritize:**

- Owners.
- Employees.
- Branded vehicles.
- Storefronts.
- Actual tools and equipment.
- Completed jobs.
- Relevant project progress.
- Real local surroundings.

Never present AI-generated people or projects as genuine employees, customers, or completed client work.

### Motion and interaction

Use subtle motion only when it helps explain state or improve perceived polish.

**Examples:**

- Button hover.
- Accordion expansion.
- Menu open/close.
- Small transitions between interface states.

**Avoid:**

- Continuous movement.
- Full-screen loading animations.
- Scroll hijacking.
- Back-button hijacking or any other interference with normal browser navigation (Google added this to its spam policies in April 2026).
- Autoplay video heroes.
- Motion-heavy image sliders.

Respect reduced-motion user preferences.

### Mobile navigation

- Keep navigation shallow.
- Ensure menu controls are accessible.
- Avoid obscuring primary page content.
- Place important contact actions within easy reach.
- Use sticky call or booking controls only when they demonstrably improve usability.
- Account for device safe areas.
- Ensure sticky controls do not cover forms, text, keyboard focus, or consent controls.

A persistent bottom CTA is an option, not a universal requirement.

## B5. High-converting homepage architecture

### Section 1: Header

**Include:**

- Business logo or readable brand name.
- Short navigation.
- Phone number or primary booking action.
- Accessible mobile menu.

On emergency-service websites, calling may be the dominant action.

On appointment businesses, booking may be primary.

### Section 2: Hero

**Required content:**

- Specific headline.
- Clear supporting explanation.
- Primary CTA.
- Relevant image or visual evidence.
- Concise verified credibility signal, if available.

Illustrative headline formula:

`[Specific Service] in [Primary Location]`

**Supporting formula:**

`[Who the business helps] + [customer outcome] + [verified distinguishing detail]`

**Examples:**

- Plumbing Repairs & Water Heater Service in Rockford.
- Roofing Repairs and Replacements Across the Stateline.
- Residential Property Management for Rockford-Area Owners.
- Wheel Alignment and Suspension Service in Loves Park.

Avoid invented urgency, promises, or response times.

### Section 3: Trust bar

Use two to four verified elements.

**Examples:**

- Genuine review rating and source.
- Real license or certification.
- Legitimate operating history.
- Recognized membership.
- Publicly verifiable company achievement.

If proof is unavailable, omit it and prioritize actual business identity.

### Section 4: Core services

Present the primary services in understandable categories.

**For each:**

- Service name.
- Customer problem.
- Short explanation.
- Reason to contact.
- Relevant CTA or detailed page link.

Avoid broad, vague service lists that obscure the actual work the company performs.

### Section 5: Why choose this business

Identify concrete differentiators.

**Examples:**

- Specific equipment expertise.
- Transparent diagnostic process.
- Verified specialty qualifications.
- Extended service hours.
- Property owner reporting.
- Experience with particular building types.

Avoid interchangeable claims such as:

- Best service.
- Highest quality.
- Unmatched excellence.
- Customer satisfaction is our priority.

### Section 6: Work examples

Show real completed work.

For each project, ideally include:

- Service performed.
- Location at an appropriate geographic level.
- Original problem.
- Work completed.
- Useful photo.
- Result, when documented.

Never invent project outcomes.

### Section 7: How it works

Use three to four simple steps.

**Contractor example:**

1. Contact the team.
2. Discuss the problem and scheduling.
3. Receive inspection or estimate information.
4. Approve the work and proceed.

Use only steps that match the business’s real process.

### Section 8: Reviews

Feature relevant, authentic feedback.

Prefer comments that clarify:

- Reliability.
- Communication.
- Problem resolution.
- Professionalism.
- Customer experience.

Link to the original review source where appropriate.

### Section 9: Service area

Clearly explain where the business actually operates.

Use a concise city list or regional description.

Avoid keyword repetition.

**Include:**

- Main base or service region.
- Primary towns.
- Boundaries or availability conditions.
- A clear way to confirm coverage.

### Section 10: FAQs

Answer genuine customer questions.

**Examples:**

- What areas do you serve?
- How are appointments scheduled?
- Do you provide estimates?
- What services do you specialize in?
- What information should I have ready?
- What happens after requesting a quote?

Answers must be based on client-confirmed policies.

### Section 11: Final CTA

Reinforce the primary service and next action.

**Example:**

Need Help With Your Furnace? Contact Our Rockford Team.

Provide phone or form access without introducing a new, confusing conversion goal.

### Section 12: Footer

**Include:**

- Business name.
- Phone.
- Email when appropriate.
- Public address if applicable.
- Service area.
- Hours.
- Navigation.
- Privacy policy.
- Appropriate license information.

For home-based service-area businesses, do not publish a private street address merely to satisfy an arbitrary NAP checklist.

## B6. Conversion and form design

### Primary CTA selection

Choose the action based on how the business wins customers.

| Business type | Likely primary action |
|---|---|
| Emergency plumbing | Call |
| HVAC repair | Call or request service |
| Roofing | Request estimate |
| Auto repair | Book appointment or call |
| Landscaping | Request quote |
| Salon | Book appointment |
| Residential property management | Request management consultation |
| Real estate agent | Schedule consultation or request property information |

### CTA design rules

- Describe the action clearly.
- Use visible contrast.
- Make it keyboard accessible.
- Use a practical touch target.
- Repeat the action where context makes it useful.
- Keep meaningful alternatives available without making the page confusing.

**Examples:**

- Call for Plumbing Service.
- Request a Roofing Estimate.
- Book an Alignment.
- Ask About Property Management.
- Schedule an Appointment.

Avoid claiming free estimates unless the client actually provides them.

### Contact forms

Ask only for information needed at the first inquiry.

**A typical contractor form:**

- Name.
- Phone or email.
- Service needed.
- Short message.

Depending on the business, request a postal code or preferred contact method.

For property management consultation forms:

- Name.
- Contact method.
- Number or type of properties.
- Brief description of needs.

Do not request sensitive identity details, bank information, Social Security numbers, or tenant application information through a generic lead form.

### Essential form behavior

- Visible labels.
- Clear required/optional indicators.
- Appropriate input types and autocomplete.
- Client-side usability checks.
- Server-side validation.
- Spam protection.
- Accessible error messages.
- Clear successful-submission confirmation.
- Actual delivery to the correct recipient.
- A fallback contact method.

The form must not show success unless the backend has accepted the submission.

### Conversion event distinction

**Track separately:**

- Phone-link click.
- Form submission accepted.
- Appointment booking completed.
- Qualified inquiry confirmed.
- Customer acquired.

A phone-link click is not proof that a call connected.

A submitted form is not automatically a qualified lead.

A qualified lead is not automatically a paying customer.

## B7. Local SEO strategy

### Keyword research

Begin with customer intent, not keyword volume alone.

**Build query groups:**

#### Category

- Plumber Rockford.
- Roofing contractor Rockton.
- Property manager Rockford.

#### Specific service

- Sump pump installation Roscoe.
- Furnace repair Loves Park.
- Wheel alignment Machesney Park.

#### Problem

- Basement drain backing up.
- Furnace blowing cold air.
- Rental maintenance coordination.

#### Commercial modifier

- Repair.
- Replacement.
- Installation.
- Estimate.
- Appointment.
- Emergency, only when genuinely offered.

**Geography**  
Use specific municipalities naturally.

Do not presume every customer searches using county names or ZIP codes.

### Keyword prioritization

Evaluate each keyword cluster by:

- Service relevance.
- Customer intent.
- Local demand evidence.
- Competitive difficulty.
- Commercial value.
- Available business proof.
- Suitability for an existing or proposed page.

For a one-page site, prioritize the main service category and core location.

For expanded sites, assign separate pages to distinct service intent.

### Title tags

Use clear, unique, relevant titles.

**Example:**

`Furnace Repair in Rockford, IL | Example Heating`

Do not mechanically stuff every city into the title.

There is no universal character limit that guarantees a title will display completely.

### Meta descriptions

Write helpful search snippets.

**Example:**

Need furnace repair in Rockford? Learn about Example Heating’s repair services, coverage area, and scheduling options. Contact the team today.

Google may use a meta description as a source for a search snippet, but may instead generate a snippet from page content. [Google's snippet guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/snippet) (clarified Nov. 6, 2026). Write descriptions to accurately summarize the page and help searchers; do not promise that the description will be used verbatim.

### Headings

- Use a clear primary heading.
- Structure sections logically.
- Keep headings descriptive.
- Avoid using headings merely for styling.
- Do not repeat geographic keywords unnaturally.

A single primary H1 is a sensible authoring convention, not a magic ranking requirement.

### Service pages

A valuable service page should include:

- Clear service explanation.
- Problems addressed.
- Customer scenarios.
- Relevant equipment or methods.
- Qualifications.
- Real project examples.
- FAQs.
- Service area relevance.
- Contact action.

Avoid generating dozens of service pages from interchangeable AI templates.

### Geographic pages

Create a separate city page only when it contains useful, genuinely location-specific information.

**Examples:**

- Real completed projects in the town.
- Local service procedures or conditions.
- Relevant geographic coverage.
- Customer-specific availability.
- Genuine location-based FAQs.
- Local team or staffed office details, when accurate.

Never invent an office to target a town.

Never claim a service-area location is a physical storefront.

Avoid pages that differ only by city name.

### Internal linking

Ensure important pages are accessible through normal HTML links.

**Examples:**

- Homepage to core services.
- Service page to related service.
- Service page to relevant project.
- Location page to appropriate services.
- All important pages to contact options.

Use descriptive anchor text.

### Technical indexability

**Check:**

- HTTP status codes.
- Correct canonical URLs.
- Appropriate robots directives.
- XML sitemap.
- Internal links.
- Rendered page content.
- Mobile usability.
- Redirect chains.
- Broken pages.

**Important distinction:**

robots.txt controls crawling; it is not a reliable method for keeping sensitive pages out of search results ([Google's robots.txt guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/robots/intro)).

Password-protect staging environments.

Use proper noindex or access controls for private content.

### Existing website redesigns

**Before changing URLs:**

- Export the current sitemap.
- Crawl existing pages.
- Identify pages receiving organic traffic.
- Identify backlinks to important URLs.
- Record current title tags and content.
- Preserve valuable URLs when possible.
- Create a URL-to-URL redirect map.
- Test permanent redirects.
- Update internal links and canonicals.
- Preserve domain ownership and Search Console history.
- For a move to a different domain or subdomain, follow [Google’s site-move guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/site-move-with-url-changes) and use Change of Address when applicable. It is not for HTTPS, www/non-www, or path changes on the same domain (clarified Nov. 6, 2026).

Avoid redirecting unrelated deleted pages to the homepage.

Do not launch a redesign without understanding what existing search visibility might be lost.

## B8. Google Business Profile integration

### Understand the distinction

**Organic search visibility**  
Influenced by the relevance, quality, usability, and broader authority of individual pages and sites.

**Google Maps/local results**  
Google says local results are based mainly on relevance, distance, and prominence/popularity—not a complete ranking formula ([Improve Local Rankings](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en); clarified Nov. 6, 2026).

These systems interact but are not identical.

### Recommended GBP audit

**Verify:**

- Business name.
- Primary category.
- Relevant secondary categories.
- Accurate public address or service-area setup.
- Business hours.
- Phone.
- Website link.
- Relevant services.
- Business description.
- Authentic images.
- Reviews and responses.
- Booking links when supported.

### Critical service-area business rule

If a business travels to customers and does not serve customers at its own address:

- Use the appropriate service-area business configuration.
- Hide the address from public display.
- Do not invent a storefront.
- Do not use a virtual office to manufacture a local presence.

As of Nov. 6, 2026, Google allows up to 20 service areas, does not support a radius, and says the overall area generally should be within about two hours’ driving time of the business base ([Manage Service Areas](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481?hl=en)). Hide the address only when customers are not served there; an eligible hybrid business that serves customers at its address may show it ([Business Profile eligibility and representation](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en)).

### Website-to-GBP consistency

Keep public business details accurate across platforms.

**Important:**

Consistency means representing the same real business information, not blindly copying every field.

A private street address hidden from customers should not automatically appear in the public website footer or schema.

### Ranking expectations

**Do not promise:**

- Number-one Maps placement.
- Rankings in every surrounding town.
- Immediate improvement from category changes.
- A fixed increase from uploading photos.
- Ranking gains from weekly GBP posts.

Maintain and improve the profile for customer usefulness and accuracy.

### Review acquisition

**A compliant process:**

1. Complete the customer service.
2. Request honest feedback.
3. Provide the direct review link.
4. Allow customers to express positive or negative experiences.
5. Do not offer incentives for Google reviews ([Google review policy](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122?hl=en)).
6. Avoid selectively requesting reviews from happy customers.
7. Respond appropriately without exposing private customer information.

Do not pressure customers to mention specific employees or keywords.

Clarification (Nov. 6, 2026): The no-incentive approach is Google’s rule for Google reviews and Clockout’s conservative practice. The FTC rule prohibits incentives conditioned expressly or implicitly on positive or negative review sentiment; other incentives may be allowed subject to disclosure and other requirements ([FTC guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)). Keep review requests neutral and do not gate them by expected rating.

### Review automation

**Advanced optional service:**

- Trigger from a documented completed-job event.
- Confirm lawful messaging permission.
- Prevent duplicates.
- Respect opt-outs.
- Measure delivery and actual review outcomes.
- Use neutral review-request language.

Do not include complex review automation in an inexpensive core website package by default.

## B9. Structured data and search appearance

### Recommended implementation

Use valid JSON-LD when appropriate.

**Typical business markup:**

- LocalBusiness.
- The most specific applicable Schema.org business subtype.
- Organization information where useful.
- BreadcrumbList for relevant multi-page structures.

Include only accurate, relevant properties.

**Possible fields:**

- Website.
- Logo.
- Public phone number.
- Business type.
- Public business address when applicable.
- Opening hours.
- Real service areas.
- Verified social profiles.

### Critical 2026 guidance

#### LocalBusiness schema

- Helps describe a real business.
- Does not automatically produce a Knowledge Panel.
- Does not guarantee better rankings.
- Google’s LocalBusiness rich-result eligibility requires a physical address; use only a real, accurate address and never invent one to qualify ([LocalBusiness structured data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business); clarified Nov. 6, 2026).

#### Review and AggregateRating schema

- Do not implement self-serving review markup expecting Google star snippets for the business’s own website.
- Google’s self-serving review restriction also applies when reviews are embedded through a third-party widget ([Review Snippet structured data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/review-snippet); clarified Nov. 6, 2026).
- Google prohibits fake reviews and incentives for Google reviews; see B8 for the distinction between Google policy and the FTC rule.

#### FAQPage schema

- FAQ content remains useful to customers.
- Google Search stopped showing FAQ rich results on May 7, 2026. As of Nov. 6, 2026, FAQ is absent from the current Rich Results Test and Search Console supported-type lists; Google does not state a separate exact removal date for those tool lists ([Search documentation updates](https://developers.google.com/search/updates); [supported search appearance types](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7552505)).
- Do not promise or sell FAQ rich results. FAQPage markup that accurately matches visible FAQ content may remain, but it does not create a Google FAQ rich result.

#### Service schema

- Schema.org Service may describe a service where appropriate, but it is not a Google special Search rich-result type ([Google supported search appearance types](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7552505); [Schema.org Service](https://schema.org/Service); clarified Nov. 6, 2026).

### Validation

**Use:**

- Google Rich Results Test for currently supported Google rich-result types; its list omits FAQ as of Nov. 6, 2026.
- Schema.org validator for general Schema.org syntax and structure.
- Manual verification that structured data matches public page content.

A valid schema implementation must never be presented as proof of future search appearance.

## B13. Performance engineering and Core Web Vitals

### Official performance thresholds

Evaluate real-user field data at the 75th percentile, segmented by device when available ([Google Core Web Vitals](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-web-vitals); clarified Nov. 6, 2026).

| Metric | Good threshold |
|---|---|
| Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) | 2.5 seconds or less |
| Interaction to Next Paint (INP) | Less than 200 milliseconds in Google Search Central guidance; Search Console’s “Good” classification uses 200 milliseconds or less |
| Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS) | 0.1 or less |

These are Core Web Vitals targets, not mandatory launch guarantees.

For new or low-traffic websites, field data may be insufficient.

PageSpeed Insights combines Chrome field data with Lighthouse lab diagnostics. For new or low-traffic sites without representative field data, use the lab diagnostics to investigate issues ([PageSpeed Insights overview](https://developers.google.com/speed/docs/insights/v5/about); clarified Nov. 6, 2026).

### Image rules

- Prefer AVIF or WebP where appropriate.
- Use responsive source sizes.
- Specify image dimensions.
- Compress realistically.
- Prioritize the hero/LCP image.
- Avoid lazy-loading the LCP image.
- Lazy-load suitable below-fold media.
- Avoid giant decorative background videos.

### Font rules

- Use as few font families and weights as necessary.
- Prefer local or appropriately optimized assets.
- Prevent layout shifts caused by fonts.
- Avoid excessive web font downloads.

### JavaScript rules

- Use JavaScript only when required.
- Defer noncritical functionality.
- Avoid large third-party widget bundles.
- Remove unused libraries.
- Do not introduce client-side rendering dependencies for ordinary static content.

### Third-party scripts

**Question each addition:**

- Analytics.
- Chat widgets.
- Review widgets.
- Scheduling embeds.
- Maps.
- Social feeds.
- Advertising trackers.

**Ask:**

1. Does it produce meaningful value?
2. Can a simpler link achieve the same result?
3. Does it harm performance or accessibility?
4. Does the client understand its ongoing cost?

### Quality targets

**Practical internal targets:**

- Lighthouse mobile performance near or above 90 under repeatable test conditions.
- No critical accessibility violations.
- Good Core Web Vitals where measurable.
- No serious unexpected layout shifts.
- Fast primary content rendering.
- Functional low-bandwidth experience.

A Lighthouse score of 90 is an internal quality goal, not a Google ranking requirement.

Never sacrifice critical functionality solely to improve a synthetic score.

## B14. Accessibility standard

Use WCAG 2.2 Level AA as a technical conformance target where feasible and appropriate ([WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)). WCAG is not a universal legal requirement for every local business; applicable legal duties depend on the jurisdiction and facts (clarified Nov. 6, 2026).

### Key requirements

- Semantic headings and landmarks.
- Keyboard-accessible interactions.
- Visible focus states.
- Labels for every form input.
- Accessible error feedback.
- Correct alt text.
- Adequate text contrast.
- Responsive content at zoom.
- No keyboard traps.
- Clear navigation.
- Reduced-motion support.
- Content that remains usable without color alone.
- Sticky content that does not obscure focus or essential page elements.

### Contrast

**WCAG 2.2 Level AA contrast criteria include:** ([Contrast Minimum](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html))

- At least 4.5:1 for normal text.
- At least 3:1 for qualifying large text.

### Target sizes

WCAG 2.2 Success Criterion 2.5.8 (Level AA) sets a 24-by-24 CSS pixel minimum target size, subject to defined exceptions including spacing between targets ([Target Size (Minimum)](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html); clarified Nov. 6, 2026).

For primary buttons and touch controls, a larger practical design target of approximately 44 to 48 pixels is often preferable; this is a design recommendation, not the AA minimum.

Do not present 48 pixels as the WCAG 2.2 Level AA minimum.

### Testing

**Use:**

- axe DevTools.
- WAVE.
- Lighthouse accessibility checks.
- Keyboard-only navigation.
- Browser zoom and reflow testing.
- VoiceOver or another screen reader.
- Real-device interaction.

Automated testing cannot prove complete accessibility compliance.

## B18. Analytics and business outcome measurement

### Essential distinction

Website events are not necessarily business outcomes.

A click-to-call event is not a completed call.

A form submission is not necessarily a qualified inquiry.

A booking click is not necessarily a confirmed appointment.

### Recommended measurement hierarchy

#### Level 1: Technical

- Site uptime.
- Broken links.
- Form reliability.
- Mobile usability.
- Core Web Vitals.

#### Level 2: Discovery

- Search Console impressions.
- Organic clicks.
- Search queries.
- Landing pages.
- Business Profile interactions when available.

#### Level 3: Engagement

- Phone-link clicks.
- Form submissions.
- Booking completion events.

#### Level 4: Business

- Qualified leads.
- Scheduled appointments.
- Completed estimates.
- Paying customers.
- Revenue and contribution profit, if the client tracks them.

### Recommended baseline

For redesigns, collect available historical data before making changes.

Prefer several months where possible.

For new websites, establish the baseline after launch and avoid promising immediate comparisons to nonexistent history.

### Core formulas

#### Website lead-action rate

`Lead actions / relevant website sessions × 100`

#### Qualified lead rate

`Qualified inquiries / total actual inquiries × 100`

#### Lead-to-customer rate

`New customers / qualified inquiries × 100`

#### Contribution ROI estimate

`(Incremental contribution profit attributable to the project - total project cost) / total project cost × 100`

Attribution is often incomplete.

Do not claim causation without sufficient evidence.

### 30-day assessment

- Verify production tracking.
- Check forms and contact flow.
- Monitor indexing status.
- Identify unexpected traffic drops.
- Review technical errors.
- Record initial inquiry data.

### 60-day assessment

- Review search queries.
- Compare service-page traffic.
- Review lead quality.
- Identify content gaps.
- Assess response workflows.
- Determine whether site structure needs adjustment.

### 90-day assessment

- Evaluate meaningful trends.
- Compare reasonably similar periods.
- Account for seasonality.
- Review actual inquiries and customers.
- Identify the highest-value next improvement.

Avoid attributing every short-term fluctuation to the redesign.

For low traffic, prioritize qualitative evidence and longer observation windows over unreliable statistical claims.

## E7. Outdated or mistaken practices to avoid

The following beliefs and practices are common in older SEO and web design material. Do not apply them.

### Outdated practice 1: Using Google Optimize for testing

Google Optimize was discontinued in September 2023.

Use a currently supported experiment tool only when appropriate.

For low-traffic local sites, prioritize usability improvements and reliable lead tracking before complex A/B tests.

### Outdated practice 2: Using Google’s Mobile-Friendly Test to validate pages

The standalone Mobile-Friendly Test and associated reporting were retired in December 2023.

Use Lighthouse, browser testing, PageSpeed Insights, and real devices.

### Outdated practice 3: Adding AMP for a mobile SEO advantage

AMP is not a general requirement for mobile ranking success. AMP content ranks like any other web page.

Do not add AMP merely for SEO.

### Outdated practice 4: Using FAQ schema to earn rich results

Google restricted FAQ rich-result eligibility in 2023 and stopped showing FAQ rich results on May 7, 2026 ([Search documentation updates](https://developers.google.com/search/updates)). FAQ is also absent from current supported-type lists; Google has not stated a separate exact date for the Rich Results Test and Search Console list changes (clarified Nov. 6, 2026).

Write FAQs for users first.

Existing FAQPage markup that accurately matches visible content does not need to be removed, but it must not be sold as a ranking or rich-result tactic.

### Outdated practice 5: Adding review ratings to LocalBusiness schema to earn stars

Google’s self-serving review restrictions apply to businesses displaying their own reviews.

Do not promise review-star rich results.

### Outdated practice 6: Treating missing LocalBusiness schema as a major competitive SEO defect

Appropriate markup is useful technical context, but missing schema does not by itself prove a ranking or conversion problem.

Prioritize real content, crawlability, contact functionality, and overall site quality.

### Outdated practice 7: Publishing exactly the same street address everywhere

Businesses that do not serve customers at their address should follow service-area business rules, including hiding private addresses where required.

Accurate public representation takes priority over mechanical NAP copying.

### Outdated practice 8: Expecting fixed ranking gains from frequent GBP posts

No universal causal improvement from posting frequency is established by Google’s published local ranking guidance.

Use updates for relevant customer communication.

### Outdated practice 9: Requiring a Lighthouse score above 90 to launch

A score near or above 90 is a useful internal target, not a universal compliance threshold.

Primary functionality, accessibility, and trustworthy business information take precedence.

### Outdated practice 10: Applying generic lead-growth statistics as universal conversion improvements

Do not apply generic benchmarks as guaranteed outcomes for local Clockout clients.

Treat conversion performance as business-specific.

### Outdated practice 11: Requiring all pages to be indexed immediately at launch

Google controls crawling and indexing, and submitting sitemaps does not guarantee inclusion.

At launch, verify that indexing is technically possible and monitor the outcome afterward.

### Outdated practice 12: Assuming more pages automatically produce better SEO

Page count should follow distinct customer intent, meaningful content, and the project’s economic justification.

Avoid scaled, repetitive, low-value pages.

## E8. Research limitations

This playbook does not establish:

- Current local search rankings for every business.
- Verified conversion rates for named competitors.
- Current performance scores for all named websites.
- Precise keyword volumes in individual Winnebago County municipalities.
- Whether a specific prospect has the budget to purchase a website.
- Whether particular automation products are included in a client’s current plan.
- A guaranteed conversion improvement from any visual or technical tactic.
- That any site will rank first in Google organic results or Maps.
- That a specific number of leads or customers will result from a launch.

Those facts must be researched or measured for individual projects.

## Pre-Sale Demo Mode

Start with the [demo brief and founder gates](website-sales-operating-system.md#demo-brief-review-and-release). An approved demo contains exactly two intentional pages. Use these quality standards at demo scale.

- **Business landing page.** Make the service, geography and primary action apparent on mobile. Choose a composition, imagery and copy that reflect the business. Clearly label the unofficial concept and simulate the inquiry flow and scripted chatbot locally without collecting personal information or sending anything.
- **Your Growth Opportunities.** Show the strongest supported findings and the corresponding design changes. ## FINAL OPERATING CONTRACT FOR CLOCKOUT’S AI AGENTS

Apply these rules to every local business website project.

### Before building

- Obtain explicit founder construction approval for the exact scope. Use the [demo gates](website-sales-operating-system.md#demo-brief-review-and-release) for pre-sale work.
- Understand the customer and business.
- Verify public information.
- Research the actual competition.
- Identify the primary customer action.
- Define the smallest commercially useful scope.
- Plan useful content and credible proof.

### While building

- Use a distinctive, client-specific visual system.
- Design mobile-first.
- Favor semantic, accessible HTML.
- Prefer lightweight architecture.
- Build a reliable contact experience.
- Include accurate local SEO foundations.
- Avoid unnecessary libraries and integrations.
- Never fabricate business claims or customer evidence.

### Before publishing

- Verify approval for the intended release. Use the applicable preview grant for a demo and separate production launch approval for a paid site.
- Verify all factual content.
- Test on mobile and desktop.
- Test keyboard and accessibility basics.
- Test every important contact path.
- Verify security and HTTPS.
- Check SEO/indexing configuration.
- Preserve existing URLs or redirects as appropriate.
- Verify final production behavior.

### After publishing

- Confirm client access and ownership.
- Monitor the live website.
- Record search and inquiry performance when included in scope.
- Distinguish website events from genuine qualified leads.
- Prioritize improvements using actual evidence.
- Recommend optional services only when they have a credible business justification.

### Definition of a successful Clockout website

A Clockout website is complete when it is:

1. Accurate: All business-specific claims are confirmed.
2. Clear: Customers immediately understand what is offered.
3. Credible: Trust is supported by authentic evidence.
4. Distinctive: Design reflects the actual business.
5. Responsive: The site works across relevant devices.
6. Accessible: Essential customer interactions are usable.
7. Functional: All contact and booking actions operate reliably.
8. Search-ready: Public content follows sound technical SEO practices.
9. Maintainable: Ownership and update procedures are documented.
10. Measurable: The client has a practical way to assess relevant business outcomes within the agreed scope.

Final principle: Build the smallest exceptional website that solves the client’s real customer-acquisition problem. Expand only when evidence demonstrates that additional investment creates meaningful value.
