# Website sales operating system

Founder decision D21 / U07 adopts the demo-first process for new website prospecting. Phase 1 documents it. Live execution remains on hold until the applicable controls are verified and Donovin authorizes the action. The [website offer](website-offer.md) owns proposed commercial terms. [Current state](../CURRENT_STATE.md) owns business priorities and dated facts.

**Website acquisition:** Research → qualify → audit → prepare a two-page brief → founder build approval → build and QA → founder preview publication approval → verify the released preview → founder outreach approval → contact → discovery and written scope → paid delivery.

Use the [working forms](../10-reference-and-archive/templates/website-project-forms.md) to record evidence at each gate. The [website playbook](clockout_website_playbook_2026.md#pre-sale-demo-mode) governs demo design and QA. The [execution system](../07-tools-and-systems/execution-system.md#demo-authorization-and-enforcement) defines how gates must be enforced.

**Historical sequence (U02, October 29):** The cash sprint favored qualifying and contacting first, then making a small concept after interest. That acquisition sequence is superseded for new website prospects. Keep the sprint's original dates, financial objective and actual outcomes separate from this decision.

For future implementation, use the [Demo Factory specification](demo-factory-spec.md) and the [phase roadmap](../09-execution-and-decisions/clockouthq-2-0-implementation.md).

## Qualification score and next action

Score five factors 0–2: customer-task friction, business relevance, reachable decision-maker, feasible scope/capacity, and plausible economic significance. A 2 needs evidence; 1 means uncertain; 0 means absent or unsuitable. Record confidence and missing facts. Suggested thresholds: 7–10 investigate first, 4–6 hold for missing information, 0–3 decline. This is a proposed prioritization aid, not a calibrated predictor of sales. A high score never overrides a harmful request, missing permission, unavailable capacity or unworkable ownership. A good website may support a different operational opportunity; route to the [general sales playbook](sales-playbook.md).

## Find and qualify ten candidates

Reuse the original sprint’s bounded research method after confirming the current objective. Use the [prospect form](../10-reference-and-archive/templates/website-project-forms.md) with the chosen tracker. Keep one record per business. The historical sprint tracker is a template. Search one town and one trade at a time:

1. **Google Maps:** search `[trade] near [town]`; record the listing, name, address/service area, category, published site and visible contact. A missing website button is a lead to verify, not proof of no site. A map result is not evidence the business is a paying prospect. If a listing appears permanently closed, exclude unless independently confirmed active.
2. **Google Search:** search the exact business name in quotation marks plus town; repeat without quotes or with owner/trade if necessary. Review the visible results and opened business-controlled pages for alternate spellings, old domains and redirects. Record search date, selected queries and candidate links. Search results can be incomplete or personalized.
3. **Directories:** inspect public chamber/trade directories for independent businesses and compare contact and domain details with business-controlled sources. Directory claims may be stale; verify them before using in a message. Do not harvest data in violation of terms.
4. **Public social pages:** inspect the public profile's link/about/contact section. A functioning social profile is not necessarily a standalone site; note it accurately. Do not use private material, scrape prohibited content, or infer personal details.
5. **Official website:** open the listed domain, note final redirect and business identity. Compare phone, location and service with the listing. A parked, expired or suspicious domain is **unverified**, not definitively absent; do not interact with warnings or submit forms.
6. **Referrals:** record leads from introductions already offered with consent. Asking for an introduction or contacting a referred prospect is outside research-only work and requires applicable outreach approval. Record permission before using a referrer’s name.

For a candidate with no verified site after these checks, say “I couldn't find a website linked from your profile or in my search on [date]” and record website status as unknown. Ask the business only after outreach approval. If an official site is verified, use the audit below. “Outdated” means a dated observation such as incorrect mobile layout or unclear current services, not a subjective visual judgment. **Batch example:** collect ten fictional or real distinct candidates; record completeness and 0–2 scores for the five factors already defined above; research the highest suitable few and place the rest in hold/decline. Score missing facts as 1 only if genuinely uncertain, not as confirmed. Avoid sending until contact identity and message basis are checked. A high score does not override a disqualifier or capacity limit.

## Audit tasks and severity

| Task | How to observe | Record / interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Identify offer | In 10 seconds find what is sold and where | Exact text/page; “unclear to reviewer” is subjective. |
| Contact | Find a phone/email or form, then check whether link leads to the stated action | Test live prospect forms only with permission. Broken `tel:` link is observed; lost calls are unknown. |
| Mobile | View around 375–390 px and a normal desktop width, scroll to bottom | Viewport/browser and overlap, horizontal scrolling, unreadable content or unreachable controls. |
| Navigation | Find a key service and return home; test menu and labels | Reproducible path and result. A minor color preference is cosmetic. |
| Trust | Compare business identity, real contact, images and evidence to sources | Missing information is not evidence of dishonesty. Never invent licenses/reviews. |
| Accessibility | Tab through controls; check focus, form labels, readable contrast, image alternatives and headings | Note manual method and defect; this is not a WCAG compliance finding or certification. |
| Performance | Use PageSpeed Insights or browser measurements when appropriate; note device mode, URL, date and test conditions | A lab snapshot is not real-user data. Prioritize a measurable barrier over chasing a score. |
| Local search | Search business name and location; compare public profile details to site | Record the query, date and discrepancies; rankings vary and no placement is guaranteed. |

Severity: **blocker** prevents the agreed critical task; **important** substantially impairs clarity or usability; **cosmetic** is mainly presentation. Confidence: observed directly / reported / unverified. For each issue record evidence, task, severity, plausible consequence and uncertainty. A short free look should choose at most one or two material issues, not promise a complete technical audit.

## Demo brief, review and release

1. Assemble a written brief from the qualified lead and dated audit. Describe both pages, the most useful customer action, evidence and gaps, design direction, asset rights and an effort cap. Stop before creating any website source, component, scaffold or reusable master template.
2. Obtain Donovin’s explicit build approval for the exact brief and scope. Build only the approved demo and review both pages for visual quality, factual accuracy, accessibility, navigation and simulated interactions. Hold or fix blocking issues before release.
3. For the pilot, obtain separate approval to publish the exact reviewed build to a named preview destination, then verify the live preview. Future build approvals may expressly include publication to a named, preconfigured preview environment under the execution policy.
4. Prepare the exact outreach package and obtain separate approval for the sender, recipient, channel, content and approved preview link. Record only verified contact outcomes. Use the discovery and paid-delivery process after interest and a written agreement.

## Conversations and response paths

These are proposed scripts. Obtain separate outreach approval before any first contact or follow-up, including phone and in-person outreach. Commercial emails need a truthful sender and subject, clear promotional identification as applicable, a valid postal address, and an easy opt-out. The FTC says the opt-out mechanism must remain available for at least 30 days and requests must be honored within 10 business days; check current federal/state and channel rules before use. [FTC guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business).

**Phone, after confirming business contact:** “Hi, I'm Donovin with Clockout in the Stateline area. I noticed [specific true observation] while looking for [service]. Are you the right person to ask about the website, or is there someone else I should speak with? I have one small idea; would it be useful to share?” If not the person, ask for the appropriate business channel and do not pressure for personal details. If busy, ask whether and when a brief follow-up is welcome. If not interested, thank them, mark do-not-contact and stop. If interested, ask what the site should help customers do and offer a short conversation; don't deliver a diagnosis over an interrupted call. If voicemail is appropriate: identify yourself, one factual reason for calling and a business callback route; don't claim results.

**Email, complete example to customize (commercial message):**

> Subject: A website concept for [business]
> Hi [name], I’m Donovin from Clockout. I noticed [verified observation] while looking for [service]. I put together a short, unofficial website concept that shows how [specific customer task] could be easier. Here it is [approved preview link]. If it’s relevant, would a short conversation be useful? This is a promotional note about Clockout’s website services. Reply “no thanks” to opt out.
> Donovin Sims, Clockout
> [verified business contact] · [valid physical postal address]

Send only the approved message after verifying the recipient, sender, preview, address and opt-out handling. If the recipient says yes, arrange the next step and show only what was promised. If they ask for price, establish scope and say the $500 introductory example is proposed and subject to a written quote. If they have an agency, offer to share the observation with permission or close the conversation. If no response, use only the separately authorized follow-up cadence and stop after the agreed number; record dates and suppress opt-outs. If address bounces, do not guess or repeatedly try personal variants.

**In person:** visit only where public access is appropriate, avoid busy periods and ask “Is there a better time to speak with whoever handles the website?” Offer a short introduction and leave immediately if unwelcome. Never present a speculative preview as an official page.

**Discovery and presentation:** ask what their main service is, who they want to reach, how inquiries currently arrive, who owns the domain/site, what is working, what frustrates staff, who approves and by when. Repeat their answer: “It sounds like the priority is [client words]; did I get that right?” Refer to the previously approved two-page preview and clarify which changes would require a separate paid agreement. Point to the observed issue, explain one possible change, ask what is inaccurate and let the client react. Do not use unauthorized copyrighted assets or fabricate customer statements. Summarize in writing and ask whether a scoped proposal is useful.

**Objections and closing:** “We have a site” → “Great; would improving [observed task] be useful, or is it already handled?” “Too expensive” → ask which outcome and budget matter; offer a separately scoped repair only if feasible and do not assume a discount. “Need to think” → ask which unanswered question would help and agree whether a follow-up is welcome. “Guarantee more leads?” → no guarantee; offer to define a measurable contact path and baseline. “Send a proposal” → verify approver, scope and required inputs, then send an offer with expiry and invite questions. “Yes” → document signed scope, exact milestones and access; a verbal yes is not a signed agreement or cash.

## Pricing, repair choice and delivery controls

For each quote estimate discovery, design, build, content cleanup, testing, client communication, one defined revision, launch and handoff in hours; add direct vendor and subcontractor costs, processing fees and a prudent contingency. Compare expected contribution and the founder's capacity with other commitments. Record price, assumptions and exclusions; the founder decides the rate and whether a job is worth accepting. Do not treat a price floor calculated here as an approved rate card. Offer a **repair** when an existing site is maintainable, the owner can authorize access and one bounded issue resolves the goal (for example, fix a contact link and test it); choose a **rebuild** only when a broader agreed need and migration risks justify it. Decline when access, ownership, security or expected work cannot be responsibly resolved.

The $500 proposal (price revised on 2026-10-31) covers a one-page site with messaging, contact CTA, essential business information, metadata, domain connection and one revision, subject to actual written terms. It excludes unagreed complex integrations, ongoing maintenance and client-paid third-party charges. $250 deposit and $250 balance are proposed, subject to client agreement and final open decisions. Before money changes hands agree: what counts as one consolidated revision; due dates and effect of missing content or access; acceptance and defect handling; who purchases domain/hosting and renewals; rights in supplied and created materials; payment, cancellation/refund and support terms; written launch authorization; and any change-order rate or new quote. Do not invent a legally enforceable contract or automatic nonrefundability.

**Workable timeline:** after scope and agreed deposit, schedule phases contingent on complete content and access. Use actual estimate and client availability, not a blanket two-day promise. On delay, log missing item, responsible party and impact, notify client and agree revised dates. For new work: describe request, quote price and schedule, and wait for written approval; retain original deliverables and acceptance rules until amended.

## Development runbook and failure recovery

- **Preflight:** obtain owner-approved copy, assets, rights and contact destination, plus domain and hosting invitations to client-owned accounts. Record project/repository location and access; never store passwords or tokens in documentation, source code or chat. Confirm existing site backup, DNS and rollback authority before changing an established site.
- **Research and outline:** verify service facts with client; define one primary customer action. Map sections, responsive layout, headings and content status. Draft copy as provisional until the client approves names, claims, hours, location and calls to action.
- **Build selection:** use Codex or OpenCode for a code-based project when a reviewable repository and hosting path suit the work; use Lovable only when its current export, account ownership, terms and deployment fit the client. Reusable starters require a verified license and removal of demo content. The tool register records costs and export assumptions. Do not create production credentials or publish without authorization.
- **Build:** run the starter locally or use a private preview, implement the smallest agreed page and contact method, make responsive decisions at narrow/wide widths, supply meaningful labels and alt text, and use real approved content. A generated dependency or third-party script needs review and a reason. Check code and licenses rather than assuming AI output works.
- **Test:** use the project QA form; inspect local build output and errors, follow keyboard focus, check navigation/links at mobile and desktop, spellcheck approved copy, verify HTTPS/metadata when deployed, and test form delivery to the named recipient only with authorization. Record tested environment and outcome; fix and rerun affected paths. A client preview is not live acceptance.
- **Approval, balance, launch:** collect consolidated revision and fix agreed defects. Document written acceptance and the agreed final-payment gate; verify settlement or another explicitly agreed milestone. Get separate authorization for deployment and DNS. Publish using client-approved accounts and retain a rollback path. Recheck public domain, TLS, links, form delivery and mobile layout after DNS propagation; record time and any unverified location or browser.
- **Handoff:** confirm the client's administrative access, domain/hosting renewals, content-edit instructions, backups, support scope and expiration. Transfer approved files and record outstanding items and acceptance. Follow up at an agreed date, with permission to measure outcomes.

If DNS is wrong, stop further changes, compare the documented original records and restore only under granted authority; warn that propagation and caches take time. If a contact form fails, do not launch it as working; fix, retest and give the client the blocker. If preview access fails, check the invitation, then use an agreed private sharing method; never lower security merely to expedite. If ownership or license cannot be verified, substitute authorized material or hold.

## Two complete fictional walkthroughs

**A. No website found — “Pine Grove Repair,” fictional.** A dated search finds a public listing and social profile with a business phone but no verified official site. Record the uncertainty and a provisional 7/10. The brief proposes a call-focused landing page and an owner report explaining the search and what is still unknown. Donovin approves that brief before construction, reviews the tested demo, separately approves preview publication, then approves the exact first email with the verified link. If the owner expresses interest, confirm facts and agree scope separately. A proposed $500 one-page scope with $250/$250 milestones would still require signed terms, payment, approved content and separate production construction and launch authorization.

**B. Poor existing site — “Riverbend Heating,” fictional.** At a recorded mobile viewport, a banner obscures the call button. Desktop navigation works. The brief proposes a visible call action and a before/proposed comparison with source and viewport; lost calls remain unknown. An approved build, QA, approved preview release and separately approved outreach precede contact. If discovery establishes that an authorized repair is a better fit, quote that repair and arrange access, backup, acceptance and rollback under written terms. No customer, outcome or sale is real.

## Weekly measurement and improvement

Count researched, qualified, approved briefs, builds, QA passes, verified contacts, replies, conversations, proposals, deposits, accepted deliveries and cleared balances for a dated window. Record demo time, rework and direct costs. Divide only where the denominator is nonzero; distinguish counts from rates. Investigate weak response by checking evidence, audience and contact route before changing price. Record actual project hours, fees, direct costs, refunds, rework and support; compare estimates with actuals. One proposed $250 deposit is below the $500 goal; two are $500 gross and create two obligations. Only usable funds after fees and holds count. The [cash manual](../08-finance-and-goals/cash-and-metrics.md) owns reconciliation. After the actual sprint review, Donovin chooses whether to continue, revise or stop this tactic.

## Tool choices and ownership evidence

Codex and OpenCode assist with reviewable code changes; use the repository's actual setup/build commands and current permissions rather than copying commands from a different stack. OpenCode's [agent documentation](https://opencode.ai/docs/agents) distinguishes Build and Plan; inspect actual configuration before allowing changes. Lovable's [ownership and hosting documentation](https://docs.lovable.dev/tips-tricks/self-hosting), reviewed 2026-10-30, explains portability and the separate responsibility for backend services. Exporting code alone does not prove the whole deployed service can be migrated. Verify the client's framework, runtime, data, integrations, billing and recovery before quoting an export or handoff. A simple brochure page should not acquire an unnecessary database or login system.
