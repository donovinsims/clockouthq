# Founder-led sales playbook
Established framework: Research → Diagnose → Build Proof → Outreach → Conversation → Implementation. Sources L01, S04/S06. Detailed operating steps below are proposed.

## Research and route proof
**Website Concept:** an observed website/customer-experience weakness.
**Visibility Snapshot:** a supported discoverability, positioning or acquisition issue.
**Lead-Handling Demo:** a validated or explicitly hypothetical response/follow-up workflow.
**Reject / No Pitch:** no meaningful evidence-backed problem.

Use the smallest relevant artifact. Label concepts as illustrative, not live client work. A public website cannot establish missed-call volume or lost revenue.

## The free look
Bound it to one meaningful bottleneck, concise evidence and one practical recommendation. The owner can then choose a deeper assessment or implementation where useful. Do not turn a free look into unlimited consulting or a mandatory paid-assessment funnel.

## Personal outreach sequence

For website prospecting, use the [website outreach sequence](website-sales-operating-system.md#conversations-and-response-paths). Its construction, preview release and outreach gates govern the generic guidance below.
1. Verify the observation and correct owner/channel.
2. Write a short message in Donovin's voice.
3. Make one modest invitation: conversation or permission to share proof.
4. Donovin sends or explicitly authorizes the message.
5. Record date, response, next action and any do-not-contact request.
6. Follow up only with relevant context. Stop when declined.

No default mass automation, autonomous Opener agent or compulsory LinkedIn campaign.

## Discovery questions
What happens today? Walk through the last real example. How often? Who handles each step? Where does it wait or fail? What is the consequence? What have you tried? What tools and permissions exist? Who approves spending and change? What would a useful result look like? Why now? What budget and deadline are realistic?

Confirm the problem in the owner's words. Separate what was observed, what they reported and what you inferred.

## Scope and close
Reflect the problem and measurable outcome. Offer the smallest feasible implementation. State boundaries and dependencies plainly. Document payment and acceptance conditions. Ask for agreement rather than using fake urgency. Do not count interest, a verbal yes, an invoice or a signed quote as collected cash.

## Common objections — proposed responses
**“I already have software.”** Review whether existing features can solve the issue before adding anything.
**“I don't want robots talking to customers.”** Start with draft-only or owner-approved wording and human escalation.
**“Will this pay for itself?”** Show assumptions and a conservative scenario; avoid guarantees.
**“Can you add this too?”** Assess the change, price and timing before accepting scope expansion.
**“I need time.”** Agree a useful next-action date or close the opportunity without pressure.

## Review
Count researched, qualified, contacted, replied, conversations, quotes, agreements, deposits, completed delivery and cleared funds separately. Learn why deals stalled. Standardize only repeated evidence.

## Pipeline record and handoff
Use the stage definitions in [execution system](../07-tools-and-systems/execution-system.md), with separate fields for client acceptance, gross payment, settlement and availability. A pipeline label must not collapse these into a single “paid” claim. Record loss/defer reasons and a dated next action.

The October 29 cash sprint previously favored confirming interest before a custom concept. For new website prospects, follow the [demo-first website procedure](website-sales-operating-system.md), including separate founder authorization before outreach. Do not pressure an uninterested owner with a full unsolicited build. Use legitimate contacts and respect no-contact requests.

## Full sales process and decision gates

This is the general proposed sales method from S04. For new website prospects, the [website sales operating system](website-sales-operating-system.md) controls the sequence and approvals. Research → diagnose → select relevant proof → personal outreach → conversation → written agreement → implementation. The free look is a bounded first conversation or observation, not a promise to deliver an entire unpaid project.

1. **Qualify:** capture source/date, actual observation, affected customer or workflow, possible impact, decision-maker and ability to deliver. Use [website procedure](website-sales-operating-system.md) for website opportunities. If no credible pain or no permission to proceed, stop.
2. **Choose proof:** an unofficial concept, visibility snapshot or lead-handling demonstration must connect to the specific observation. Clearly label illustrative work and avoid unauthorized data, logos, claims and testimonials. Ask before sharing or building more.
3. **Contact:** use a relevant business-published channel and explain why you chose them. Use the [templates](../10-reference-and-archive/templates/outreach-and-follow-up.md); record opt-outs and comply with channel requirements. Do not claim a guaranteed result or manufacture urgency.
4. **Discover:** ask for a recent example, frequency, current handling, time spent, customer consequence, owner, decision authority and constraints. Reflect back the problem, ask what success would mean and test whether an existing process or tool is enough. Estimated losses are assumptions until supported by a client baseline.
5. **Propose:** document outcomes, deliverables, exclusions, acceptance, client dependencies, access, dates, price, payment, change control and support. If price is challenged, clarify value and reduce scope only with an explicit revised agreement; do not promise arbitrary discounts or outcomes. If timing is wrong, record an agreed follow-up rather than repeatedly chasing.
6. **Handoff:** record signed acceptance, approved access, payment status, next owner and date. A verbal yes is not a contract or cleared payment. Close out no-fit or opted-out prospects respectfully.

**Measurement and exceptions:** Track stage counts with dates, follow-up due, paid conversion and actual project hours. Zero denominator means rate unavailable; replies do not equal sales. If a prospect asks for sensitive data or guaranteed legal compliance, pause and seek appropriate professional review. If no one replies, review targeting and message evidence before changing the offer; small samples do not establish causality.

## Execute the free business look

Use this procedure when an owner agrees to discuss one operational problem. Proposed working limit: 20–30 minutes plus a concise written summary; agree any exception. Bring the prospect record, one evidenced observation and a blank discovery record. Ask permission to take notes and explain that recommendations may include using existing tools or changing a process.

1. Ask: “What happened the last time this caused a problem?” Record the event, date range, affected role and consequence in the owner's words. Ask what happens when the process works normally. Avoid leading with a product.
2. Draw the actual sequence: trigger, input, responsible person, tool, handoff, decision, completion. Mark missing inputs, repeated entry, waiting and rework. Ask the person who does the work to confirm it where possible.
3. Separate symptom from cause. “Slow responses” is a symptom; missing ownership, incomplete intake or an unmonitored inbox are competing causes. Ask what evidence would distinguish them. Do not conclude that automation fixes a staffing or policy problem.
4. Estimate impact with the owner: occurrences per period × active minutes per occurrence, plus independently evidenced direct costs. Record the source, range and assumptions. Do not add hypothetical lost sales to labor savings or value saved time as guaranteed cash.
5. Compare four options: leave unchanged, clarify process/ownership, configure an existing tool, or build an integration. For each record effort, risk, cost, access and reversibility. Prefer the smallest option that addresses the supported cause.
6. Return a one-page finding: observation, evidence, likely cause and confidence, affected workflow, recommended next step, alternatives, expected measure, dependencies and unresolved questions. Ask the owner to correct inaccuracies.
7. Close with a choice: implement the simple change themselves, authorize a separately scoped assessment, discuss a paid implementation, or stop. Say: “Would it help if I scoped that next step, or would you prefer to handle it internally?” There is no mandatory paid diagnostic.

**Completion:** owner has received the finding, uncertainties are visible, and the tracker records the agreed next action or closure. If evidence is insufficient, recommend a bounded measurement period rather than a confident solution. If access, authority, economics or safe delivery is absent, decline the implementation.

**Example, fictional:** a service business reports delayed quote follow-up. Mapping shows requests arrive in two inboxes with no assigned owner. First test one daily owner and a shared queue for an agreed observation window. Only consider reminders after ownership and status definitions work. Measure requests awaiting follow-up, elapsed time and staff effort; record whether a customer actually responded separately from whether a reminder was sent.

## Response and closing decisions

| Response | Operator action | Record |
|---|---|---|
| Interested | Ask for a short discovery slot and confirm decision-maker | Agreed date and question |
| Send information | Send the promised concise finding or clearly unofficial proof; ask whether follow-up is welcome | Asset, permission and next date |
| Already has a provider | Ask whether the observed issue is already being handled; close if so | No-fit or permitted future follow-up |
| Too expensive | Ask which outcome matters and whether a smaller independent scope would help | Revised scope only if agreed |
| Needs approval | Identify approver and offer a concise scope summary | Decision process, not a presumed sale |
| No / opt out | Acknowledge and stop outreach | Suppression status |
| No reply | Follow the bounded cadence; then close or defer | Last touch and closure reason |
| Ready to proceed | Confirm scope, terms, approver and next payment/access gate in writing | Agreement and milestone evidence |

At acceptance, transfer the finding, scope, client responsibilities and unresolved risks to the delivery record. After a useful accepted result, ask permission for an introduction: “Is there another owner with a similar issue who might find this useful?” Do not imply permission to use the client's name publicly.

## Feasibility and solution decision

Before proposing paid work, test seven questions: Is the cause supported by evidence? Is the input reliable? Is the business rule defined? Can the client grant legitimate access? Can errors be detected and reversed? Is a human owner available? Do conservative value and price justify all delivery and support work? If any answer is unknown, scope the missing investigation instead of promising the result. If an answer is no, change the intervention or decline.

For a repeatable rule with stable inputs, a checklist or deterministic configuration may be adequate. For ambiguous text, AI can draft or classify with a reviewed exception queue; measure false positives and missed cases. For safety, spending, legal, housing or customer commitments, preserve the appropriate human approval. Do not automate an undefined process simply because software is available.

Proposal output must connect observed problem → chosen intervention → deliverable → acceptance test → baseline/result measure. Estimate time and direct costs, document uncertainty and use the business plan's economics framework. A free look or mentor example does not fix a price. Reject opportunities requiring unsupported guarantees, hidden access, privacy violations, capability beyond responsible delivery, or an uneconomic support burden. Record the reason and offer a narrower authorized alternative only if it helps the buyer.
