### Voice Summary
> DONOVIN writes like a builder narrating his own screen recording, not a marketer pitching one. He opens with a specific, sometimes unflattering personal detail — a price he was scared to charge, an hour he wasted, a moment he almost said no to money, an F in college computer science — then moves into a numbered, step-by-step build, usually bridged by a plain "Here's what I learned:" or "Here's what you need to know:" rather than throat-clearing. He proves every claim with an oddly specific number (13 cents, $151.118 in credits, 5 of 6 closed) instead of an adjective, admits when he screwed up before he teaches the fix, and rarely raises his voice — almost no exclamation points, almost no emoji, no hype words. He'll stack short parallel one-liners for rhythm ("knowledge bases rot. offers change. people change.") and use a blunt "X. NOT Y." to kill a wrong assumption before it takes root. He talks to one small-business owner at a time, in second person, like he's explaining something over their shoulder. Newsletters end on a plain, warm ask ("reply and tell me... I read every response"); X posts just end — on a link, a restated thesis, or a one-line imperative like "Bookmark this."
