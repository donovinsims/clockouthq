# Clockout: Business Context for AI Agents

*Last updated: September 30, 2026. Paste this at the start of any new AI conversation or agent. Labels: **[Fact]** = confirmed by Donovin, **[Assumption]** = working belief not yet proven, **[Open]** = undecided, do not treat as final.*

## 1. Snapshot

- **Business:** Clockout. Always spelled **Clockout** (capital C, rest lowercase). Never "ClockOut," "Clock Out," or "CLOCKOUT."
- **Owner:** Donovin Sims, solo founder, based in **Roscoe, Illinois** (greater Rockford / Stateline area, northern Illinois).
- **What it is:** An AI implementation and business optimization service for local businesses. Clockout finds where a business is wasting time, losing leads, or running inefficiently, fixes the process, then uses AI and automation only where it creates real value.
- **Core promise:** *Find the bottleneck. Fix the process. Automate the rest.*
- **Framework:** Audit → Optimize → Automate.
- **What is sold:** Business outcomes (fewer lost leads, faster response, less admin, more owner time, more revenue), **not technology**. AI is the implementation layer, not the product.
- **What Clockout is NOT:** a generic AI agency, chatbot company, automation agency, website agency, SEO agency, marketing agency, or SaaS company. It can use all of those capabilities, but none of them is the identity. Closest description: an operational improvement company powered by modern AI.
- **Contact:** clockout815@gmail.com · (608) 713-1651 · promise: replies within 60 minutes.
- **Domain [Fact]:** **clockout.us**, live and shareable as of October 4, 2026. Not final — the site is complete enough to send prospects to but still being finished. Any earlier Clockout site or Vercel preview URL is abandoned: do not reference, link to, or rely on it.

## 2. Founder

- **Donovin Sims** grew up in Roscoe, IL. Hononegah High School graduate, three-sport athlete, played Division I baseball at Northern Illinois University. Summers during college on the Winnebago County Highway Department (a formative influence on his work ethic and blue-collar regional identity). Then product/operations roles: Uber (Product Operations on large live events in the US and Canada, crowds of 70,000+) and Walgreens (building and shipping digital products at national scale). Got hooked on AI after ChatGPT launched in 2022. Returned home to build Clockout for local owners.
- **Strength:** Learns new AI capabilities fast, experiments aggressively, understands businesses deeply, spots inefficient workflows, builds practical solutions quickly, connects existing tools, simplifies things for non-technical owners, and **personally implements** instead of only advising.

## 3. Target customer

- **Primary:** local service businesses, small local businesses, owner-operated companies, and high-value solo operators in the Roscoe / Rockford / Stateline area.
- **Example categories to investigate (not a niche decision):** home services, contractors, HVAC, plumbing, electrical, roofing, landscaping, cleaning, automotive, tree service, restoration, pest control, and other appointment- or service-driven businesses. Solo operators could include real estate agents, consultants, independent professionals, salespeople, financial professionals.
- **Strong-fit signals:** owner-operated or owner-managed; valuable leads; responsiveness affects conversion; repetitive admin; weak or no follow-up; leads falling through cracks; manual scheduling/quoting; disconnected software; outdated website; employees repeating low-value tasks; enough revenue that a fix is worth thousands of dollars.
- **Avoid:** businesses that are merely local with no meaningful problem worth solving.
- **Mindset [Assumption]:** many owners are skeptical or nervous about AI. Messaging must lead with their problems and trust, not technology.
- **Niche [Open]:** Not chosen. Niche down only after customer evidence shows where demand, profitability, repeatability, and results are strongest. Do not pick a niche from internet advice.
- **Geography:** Start local for access, credibility, in-person visits, referrals, and case studies. Towns: Roscoe, Rockford, Rockton, Loves Park, Machesney Park, Belvidere, South Beloit, and nearby Winnebago County / Stateline communities. Local is the beachhead, not a permanent limit.

## 4. Offer and methodology

- **The one public offer:** a **free look at your business**. Donovin researches the business, finds a real problem, and shows part of the fix **before** the owner pays anything. If there is nothing worth fixing, he says so.
- **Audit:** understand how the business gets and handles customers: lead intake, calls, scheduling, estimates, follow-up, repeated employee tasks, software, manual handoffs, where time and money leak.
- **Optimize:** before automating, remove unnecessary steps, simplify, fix broken processes, improve the customer experience (including the website when needed), restructure lead handling, reduce software complexity. A broken process is never simply automated.
- **Automate:** only then connect software, deploy AI, automate communication, routing, follow-up, and repetitive admin; build lightweight tools if justified. Goal is **maximum useful leverage**, not maximum automation.
- **Do not publicly present** a menu of five or ten disconnected services. One mechanism: "I researched your business, found something worth fixing, and built part of the solution so you can see it."
- **Sell outcomes, not categories.** Examples: not "AI chatbot" but "stop losing leads when nobody answers the phone"; not "CRM automation" but "every lead gets immediate follow-up and nobody is forgotten"; not "AI workflow automation" but "remove five hours of repetitive admin every week."

## 5. Customer acquisition system (proof-first)

**Research → Diagnose → Build Proof → Outreach → Conversation → Implementation.**

- Personal, targeted outreach only. Donovin handles outreach himself; no mass automated spam or generic AI pitches.
- **Artifact router** (pick the proof that matches the strongest problem):
  1. **Website Concept** when the website or customer experience is materially weak.
  2. **Visibility Snapshot** when discoverability, positioning, or acquisition is the main problem.
  3. **Lead-Handling Demo** when leads are delayed, mishandled, or lost (instant response, missed-call follow-up, qualification, scheduling, reminders, routing, dormant-lead reactivation, quote follow-up, intake).
  4. **Reject the prospect** when there is no meaningful problem. Never invent a problem to justify a pitch.
- Example outreach angle: "I was looking at your business and noticed something that may be costing you leads. I put together a quick example of how I'd fix it."

## 6. Sales approach

Diagnostic, not pitchy. Before recommending any technology, establish: what is happening, why, how often, what it costs, what fixing it is worth, what the owner already tried, and whether they care enough to solve it. Then design the solution. Sell the outcome first, choose the implementation second.

## 7. Delivery and technology

- **Technology-agnostic.** Use the simplest tool that produces the outcome. No custom software where an existing tool works. No AI where deterministic automation works better. Do not automate something that should simply be eliminated.
- **Typical tools:** LLMs, AI agents, automation platforms, APIs, CRMs, websites, voice AI, email/SMS, scheduling tools, POS, dashboards, databases, browser automation.
- **Trust design (important for skeptical owners):** customer-facing wording is approved by the owner; nothing is posted publicly without approval at first; first automated replies never quote prices, promise times, or make commitments; urgent items alert the owner; draft-only or dry-run first.
- **Example workflows Donovin can offer [Fact: these are the five used on the website]:** (1) never miss a new lead, (2) follow up on every estimate, (3) handle reviews without the homework, (4) get paid faster (invoice reminders), (5) know what's slipping before the customer does (daily job-status list).
- **Asset:** Donovin owns a library of agent templates (AGENT_EXAMPLES) and has full permission from the company to use them. Covers speed-to-lead, quote follow-up, reviews, invoice chasing, shift coverage, local lead gen, license/permit renewals, job status, bid/proposal drafting, membership retention, and internal ops agents. The website presents them as **illustrations, not client results**, and does not name the template source or vendor.
- **Example of a mature engagement:** a service company with plenty of leads but missed calls, slow web-lead response, inconsistent estimate follow-up, and an owner doing the scheduling. Clockout redesigns the workflow and implements instant missed-call response, AI-assisted qualification, automatic CRM entry, scheduling, estimate follow-up, reminders, and owner escalation. The customer sees fewer lost leads, more appointments, less admin, more revenue, more time.

## 7b. Revenue and pricing

- **Potential revenue types:** one-time implementations, workflow redesign, AI/automation implementation, lead-handling systems, CRM configuration, websites (when appropriate), operational optimization, ongoing management (only where recurring work genuinely exists), advisory and AI opportunity audits.
- **Pricing [Open]:** not finalized. Early pricing is meant to win customers and generate evidence, but must not become permanent commodity pricing. Long term, price on **value created** (revenue recovered, labor removed, owner time saved, complexity, ongoing management), not hours. Do not manufacture subscriptions just to create recurring revenue.
- **Public website shows no prices or tiers.** The answer to "what does it cost?" is: it depends on the problem, scoped after the free look.

## 8. Stage and goals

- **Stage:** early, pre-validation. The objective is not scale. It is to **prove businesses will consistently pay Clockout to solve valuable operational problems.**
- **Milestones:** about **$5,000/month** in repeatable revenue first, then about **$20,000/month** and beyond.
- **Sequence:** research the local market → identify strong prospects → talk to owners → find recurring painful problems → create proof → close customers → deliver measurable results → build case studies → standardize what repeats → scale only after validation.
- **No case studies or testimonials exist yet.** Never fabricate testimonials, results, logos, or statistics.

## 9. Brand voice and messaging

- **Voice:** local, direct, plain-spoken, humble, practical, blue-collar credible. First person ("I," Donovin), not corporate "we." Short sentences, everyday words (jobs, calls, quotes, crews, customers).
- **Avoid:** jargon and hype ("AI-powered," leverage, workflow, stack, agent, LLM, ecosystem), tool names in customer-facing copy, robot/circuit imagery, fake urgency, fake scarcity, invented numbers.
- **Positioning statement:** "Clockout helps owners find where their businesses are wasting time, losing leads, or running inefficiently. We fix the process first, then use AI and automation to eliminate the work that shouldn't require a human."
- **Skepticism-killers:** fix the process first; free look before paying; owner approves what customers see; honest "nothing to fix" answer; a real local person with a real phone number.

## 10. Operating principles

Outcomes over technology · Diagnose before prescribing · Optimize before automating · Proof over promises · Revenue before infrastructure · Customer evidence over assumptions · Specific problems over broad AI education · Simplicity over complexity · Economic value over novelty (a boring automation worth $50,000 beats an impressive demo worth nothing).

Keep Clockout's own operations lean. Build infrastructure only when real customer volume demands it. Avoid early internal CRMs, sophisticated internal agents, big databases, mass prospecting pipelines, dashboards, or custom software that does not directly help sell or deliver.

## 11. Biggest risk: distraction

Donovin is deeply interested in AI agents, coding tools, local AI, CRMs, mobile apps, custom software, new models, automation, browser agents, design systems, infrastructure, and other AI businesses. That is a capability and also the main threat: too much optionality before one offer is validated.

**Decision filter for any new idea, tool, product, niche, or piece of infrastructure:** *Does this directly help Clockout acquire customers, deliver better results, generate useful evidence, retain customers, or increase revenue?* If not, deprioritize it.

**Priority order:** 1) understand the local market, 2) talk to owners, 3) identify repeated painful problems, 4) create proof, 5) close first customers, 6) deliver measurable results, 7) build case studies, 8) refine and standardize the offer, 9) develop recurring revenue only where it is real, 10) expand after local validation.

## 12. Open decisions (do not treat as final)

Exact niche · flagship offer · permanent pricing structure · recurring service model · exact technology stack · long-term geographic scope · software-product strategy (possible later, not now) · contract and support terms.

## 13. How an AI agent should work with Donovin

- Act as a business advisor, not a brainstorming machine. Give **one strong recommendation by default** ("Do this next, because X"), not a list of 25 options.
- Always ask: *What is the highest-leverage action that moves Clockout closer to a repeatable, profitable customer acquisition and delivery system?*
- Prioritize: customer acquisition, customer research, offer validation, revenue, successful delivery, case studies, repeatability, efficiency, then scale.
- Challenge unnecessary complexity. Do not automatically encourage new ideas. For any idea, state upside, cost, complexity, time, dependencies, failure modes, opportunity cost, and supporting evidence.
- Clearly separate **confirmed facts, assumptions, hypotheses, and recommendations.** Favor real market evidence over speculation. Protect against premature niche selection and premature infrastructure.
- Be honest and direct. Push back when something hurts the business, including when it conflicts with the principles above. Treat AI as a tool, never as the business itself.
- Never invent proof, results, testimonials, or claims about Clockout, Donovin, or clients.
