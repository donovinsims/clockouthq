# Product Marketing Context

**Document version:** v1
**Last updated:** 2026-09-30

## Product overview
Clockout is Donovin Sims's practical business improvement service. He studies how work moves through a business, simplifies the process, and implements AI or automation where it helps. Method: **Audit → Optimize → Automate**. Service business; pricing and scope follow diagnosis. No published prices or free-audit promise.

## Audience and jobs
Owner-operated businesses around Roscoe, Rockford, and the Stateline. No industry niche selected. Owner is usually buyer, user, and decision maker; team members need clear handoffs. Jobs: keep inquiries from being forgotten; reduce repeated admin; know which work needs attention. Fit depends on a recurring, worthwhile problem and access to reliable information.

## Problems and alternatives
Missed follow-up, scattered information, paperwork, and owner dependence are hypotheses supported by limited public-language research, not verified local customer interviews. Alternatives: existing software features, better checklists, staff help, or doing nothing. No unsupported competitor superiority claims.

## Difference and objections
Diagnose first; improve the process before introducing technology. Donovin implements practical changes. Start small and preserve human control. Objections: another tool to manage; robotic customer messages; poor data; unclear value. Answer with existing-tool review, draft-only workflows, explicit limits, baseline measurement, and agreed scope. Bad fit: no recurring problem, expectation of guaranteed results, or unwillingness to review consequential decisions.

## Switching dynamics
Push: repeated work and missed opportunities. Pull: a clear workflow and less chasing. Habit: memory, inboxes, notes. Anxiety: loss of control or more complexity. These are research hypotheses until conversations establish fit.

## Language and voice
Plain, personal, practical, calm. Clockout spelling follows the pasted request. Start with “Less busywork. Fewer missed opportunities.” Preserve “Let me take a look.”, “See examples.”, “Send it to Donovin.” Use owner language: inquiries, estimates, invoices, jobs, paperwork. Avoid autonomous-agent/product catalog language, guaranteed gains, fabricated statistics, and AI hype. Public quotations are attributed in research only, never turned into testimonials.
