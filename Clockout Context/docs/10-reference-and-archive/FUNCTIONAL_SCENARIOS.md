# Master v3 functional scenario walkthroughs

Documentation-only tests performed 2026-10-30. All businesses, inputs, agreements, payments and outcomes below are fictional fixtures. No search for real prospects, customer contact, actual build/deployment, receipt, launch or live validation is claimed. The test is whether an operator can produce the right record and decision from the manuals. The final structural validator separately checks links and data. Remaining commercial choices are deliberately passed to Donovin or a client rather than invented.

## 1. Research and qualify a batch of ten

Documents: website operating system, market qualification, website project forms and sprint tracker. Fixture: ten hypothetical businesses in the established Stateline geography, with assumed observations for this exercise. Apply the five 0–2 factors in the order friction/relevance/reachability/feasibility/significance. No real business facts are asserted.

| Fictional business | Fixture observation | Scores / total | Decision and output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pine Grove Repair | No site found; owner contact published; existence not confirmed | 1/2/2/2/1 = 8 | Research first; ask whether another site exists |
| Riverbend Heating | Mobile call control obstructed | 2/2/2/2/1 = 9 | Assess bounded repair; loss unknown |
| Cedar Lane Cleaning | Social-only presence; identity consistent | 1/2/2/2/1 = 8 | Ask whether site would serve a stated goal |
| Meadow Auto Care | Published number differs across directory/site | 2/2/1/2/1 = 8 | Hold outreach until contact verified |
| Quarry Fence | Site parked; active status uncertain | 1/1/1/1/1 = 5 | Hold, verify activity and domain |
| Maple Pet Care | Contact links work; only color preference | 0/1/2/2/0 = 5 | Do not pitch redesign; no supported pain |
| Willow Landscaping | Service geography absent, owner reachable | 1/2/2/2/1 = 8 | Ask whether clarity matters to inquiries |
| Prairie Appliance | Rebuild needs unavailable proprietary access | 2/2/2/0/1 = 7 | Hold despite total; resolve ownership first |
| Oak Trail Painting | Duplicated listing, same firm as another row | 1/1/1/1/1 = 5 | Deduplicate, retain one business record |
| Millstone Plumbing | Business confirmed closed in fixture | 0/0/0/0/0 = 0 | Decline |

Expected operator output: ten research records with source/query/date fields, verification status, score evidence, rejection/hold reasons and next actions; not ten automatically qualified leads. Finding: original score language could reward uncertain facts; final guide requires evidence and lets disqualifiers override totals. Result: sufficient for a real research batch once the operator supplies actual sources. No outreach authorization is inferred.

## 2. Evaluate an existing site

Documents: website audit tasks and website audit form. Input: Riverbend fixture at 390 px, menu/banner obscures call control, desktop works, revenue unknown. Output: page/device/date, reproduction steps, screenshot location, severity important or blocker according to the agreed critical task, direct observation versus inferred impact. Decision: show one relevant issue, do not submit a live form or claim lost revenue. Finding addressed: manual explicitly distinguishes a lab/manual observation from user data and compliance certification. Result: sufficient; actual measurement needs site access and observation.

## 3. Conduct a website sales conversation

Documents: website conversations, general response table, outreach template. Input: fictional owner asks “Can you guarantee more leads?” Response: no guarantee; clarify the customer task and discuss a measurable contact path. If busy, agree whether follow-up is welcome; if no, suppress further contact. Output: discovery note, permission and next action. Finding addressed: natural phone, email, in-person and objection paths exist; email placeholders must be completed and opt-outs handled. Result: sufficient; real contact requires separate authorization.

## 4. Prepare a $500 proposal

Documents: proposed website offer, pricing controls and scope/website forms. Fixture: one page, approved messaging, contact CTA, essential info, metadata, domain connection, one consolidated revision; third-party costs and maintenance excluded. Output: proposed $500 price, proposed $250/$250 milestones, client inputs, acceptance, timing dependencies, rights, support, change and cancellation terms awaiting actual agreement. Decision: $500 is a scenario, not adopted permanent pricing; no automatic nonrefundability. Finding addressed: blanks for commercial choices are intentional approval gates. Result: sufficient to prepare a reviewable proposal; Donovin/client must choose terms.

## 5. Handle agreement and upfront deposit

Documents: lifecycle gates, cash manual and payment form. Fixture: signed scope, $250 gross received, $10 fee, settlement pending. Output: signed version reference, invoice/payment status, $240 net before other obligations, available amount not yet confirmed. Decision: follow the actual agreed build gate; count zero toward an availability goal until usable funds are verified. Finding addressed: received, settled and available states are separate. Result: sufficient; payment source evidence remains external.

## 6. Build and test under the development playbook

Documents: development runbook, tool selection, QA and website intake. Fixture: authorized assets, client-owned domain/hosting, a licensed starter and repository. Walkthrough: inspect actual setup commands → build privately with Codex/OpenCode or appropriate Lovable route → review generated content/code → test responsive layout, keyboard, links, normal/error form behavior and authorized delivery → log defects and retest. Output: requirements, private draft, build/test evidence and issue log. Decision: hold a failing form and missing approval. Finding addressed: export must include backend/dependency review, not code alone. Result: sufficient documentation; no actual application was built or tested in this scenario.

## 7. Handle revision and scope change

Documents: delivery scope controls and design review form. Fixture: one included copy revision plus request for a new booking system. Output: consolidated in-scope edits and separate change request with price/time/access/acceptance effect. Decision: fix agreed defects; obtain written approval before added work. Finding addressed: distinguish defect, revision and scope addition. Result: sufficient; client agreement remains required.

## 8. Accept, collect balance and hand off

Documents: acceptance/launch runbook, cash rules and handoff form. Fixture: owner approves exact version but $250 balance is pending. Output: acceptance evidence, outstanding milestone, launch hold unless an explicit alternative was agreed. After fictional availability and launch permission, checklist requires public verification, account-access confirmation, renewals, update instructions, rollback and support limits. Decision: neither preview nor payment alone grants launch permission. Result: sufficient; actual balance, release and client acceptance require real evidence.

## 9. Conduct the free business look

Documents: sales playbook free-look procedure, client brief and prospect forms. Fixture: quote follow-up delayed; two inboxes and no assigned owner. Map trigger/input/role/tool/handoff/completion; ask for last example and evidence, compare causes, recommend named ownership and a queue before automation. Output: one-page finding, confidence, alternatives, baseline and next step. Finding addressed: the earlier summary lacked a complete root-cause procedure; final playbook supplies it. Result: sufficient; client facts require confirmation.

## 10. Evaluate a property-management opportunity

Documents: property-management manual and opportunity record. Fixture: manager reports stale vendor status but already has a work-order platform. Output: roles, workflow, current-feature review, permission/baseline questions and possible configuration pilot. Decision: do not assume a custom system or permanent niche. Finding addressed: detailed maintenance states and existing-tool-first check added. Result: sufficient for research; actual system configuration and willingness to pay remain unverified.

## 11. Decide whether to automate

Documents: sales feasibility, property-management safeguards and QA. Fixture: intake varies, emergency policy undefined, no reviewer assigned. Output: no-go for external automation, proposed process definition and reviewed manual queue. With stable ordinary inputs, defined rules, permission and recovery, a draft-only pilot could be considered. Finding addressed: reliable input, human ownership, reversibility and economics are explicit gates. Result: sufficient; undefined business rules are a client decision dependency.

## 12. Reconcile the November 10 sprint

Documents: November 10 review, cash tracker and decision record. Fixture only: two $250 gross deposits, $20 combined fees, one $40 project cost and $200 reserved for obligations yield $240 available from those receipts; balances unpaid. Decision: this fixture does not meet $500 available and creates two delivery obligations. Output: receipt/settlement/availability reconciliation, obligation list, actual stage counts and founder decision to continue/change/stop. Finding addressed: no calendar-based automatic property-management start. Result: sufficient; actual sprint success/failure is unknown without records and owner review.

## 13. Update the knowledge base after a strategy decision

Documents: maintenance SOP, decision template, source/claims registers, current state and checkpoint. Fixture: Donovin explicitly chooses a bounded property-management experiment after reviewing real sprint records. Output: dated source and decision with options/reason → canonical topic update → relevant claims/state/open-decision change → changelog and checkpoint → link/data/checksum validation and reviewed package. Preserve the earlier cash plan as dated history. Decision: a suggestion or date alone does not change strategy. Finding addressed: one canonical owner per topic and explicit source status. Result: sufficient; the fictional choice is not entered as an actual business decision.

## Overall result and remaining dependencies

All 13 documentation walkthroughs reach a defined record and decision path. The exercise uncovered and addressed unclear qualification overrides, code-only migration assumptions, symptom/root-cause handling, payment-state distinctions and the scope-change classification. No remaining ambiguity prevents preparing a reviewable artifact. Real authority, commercial terms, missing historical attachments, customer observations, payment evidence, legal advice and measured outcomes remain external dependencies; no live validation is claimed.
