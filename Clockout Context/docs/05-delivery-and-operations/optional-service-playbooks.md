# Optional and advanced service playbooks
PROPOSED adaptations of mentor guidance, not active Clockout products. Sources S08/S09/S04. Prices and revenue examples from Corey remain reference only.

## Deeper paid assessment
Use when diagnosis itself is valuable and the buyer agrees to the scope.
1. Agree purpose, fee, deliverables, access and review date.
2. Collect payment according to the agreement.
3. Conduct consent-based discovery; do not record without consent.
4. Review actual workflows, baseline, tools and constraints.
5. Identify roughly 3–7 supported opportunities when evidence warrants that range; do not pad the report.
6. Prioritize impact, effort, risk and dependency. State financial assumptions.
7. Deliver a concise report, immediate action plan and review conversation.
8. Offer implementation only where it is a fit; assessment does not obligate further purchase.

## Done-with-you / Concierge
Agree what Donovin advises on and what the customer implements. Mentor reference includes intake, two monthly calls, asynchronous communication and a shared workspace. Clockout has not adopted that package. Define cadence, time limits, response windows, backlog, ownership, renewal and cancellation before selling.

## Managed operations
Select one recurring process, identify an accountable human owner, set monitoring and exception handling, test failure/recovery, price support and third-party costs, and define exit/data handoff. Do not sell “managed agents” without a viable service obligation and capacity to maintain it.

## Workshops
Validate buyer demand and a useful practical outcome. Define audience, format, learning goals, permitted data, demonstrations, materials and follow-up consent. Base the workshop on real expertise rather than promised inbound leads. Advanced policy or leadership work needs appropriate competence and scope.

## Financial assessment discipline
Time saved = volume × minutes reduced. Cash impact requires an actual reduction in spending or measured incremental contribution. Report reclaimed capacity separately. Keep attribution conservative; a plausible ROI scenario is not a guaranteed result.

## Adoption gate
Owner decision, buyer evidence, scoped deliverable, feasible permissions, risk boundaries, viable economics and capacity must exist before changing service status to ACTIVE.

## Execute an optional engagement without implying a launched product

Use these formats only after a qualified buyer's need and Donovin's capacity justify them. Confirm purpose, deliverables, time boundary, customer responsibilities, price and acceptance before starting. No package cadence or price from Corey is automatically adopted.

- **Paid diagnostic:** use when uncertainty is substantial enough that a bounded investigation itself has value. Agree the workflow, people to interview, permitted records, sample/window and output. Map the process, verify baseline and causes, compare no-change/process/existing-tool/custom options, estimate costs and risks, then deliver findings and a recommendation. Acceptance is a usable evidence-backed decision record, not a promise of ROI or an automatic implementation sale.
- **Done-with-you:** identify who implements and who reviews; list the bounded tasks, meeting/support allowance, response windows and exclusions. Begin with one approved task and teach the client to repeat it. Record decisions and client-owned artifacts. If the customer needs execution rather than coaching, rescope instead of silently absorbing implementation into advisory time.
- **Managed operations:** define the recurring work, service owner, permitted external effects, monitoring, exception queue, recovery, review cadence and termination/handoff. Estimate recurring labor and vendor costs under normal and incident conditions before pricing. Stop onboarding more clients if monitoring/support cannot be maintained. A subscription label does not establish a viable service.
- **Workshop:** agree audience, practical objective, duration, data rules, equipment and participant access. Use synthetic examples, rehearse the exercise and provide a fallback if a tool fails. Finish with an output participants can use and a feedback/next-step record. Do not promise inbound leads or legal expertise. Follow-up marketing requires appropriate permission.

Review completion against the accepted deliverables, actual hours/costs, customer usefulness and next responsibility. When a format repeatedly creates unclear scope or weak economics, revise it before selling again. Use the delivery playbook for all payment, permissions, change, QA and handoff controls rather than maintaining a competing SOP here.
