# Delivery playbook
Principles established in S04/L01; operational steps are a PROPOSED reusable process, to be agreed per engagement.

An approved pre-sale demo is sales collateral. Use the [website sales handoff](../04-offers-and-sales/website-sales-operating-system.md#demo-brief-review-and-release) to start a separate paid engagement with written scope, payment, authorized production construction, acceptance and launch.

## 1. Diagnose
Map the actual workflow and a recent example. Identify the owner, users, inputs, tools, handoffs and failure points. Establish a baseline and measurement window. Confirm whether a process change or existing feature can solve the problem.

Output: client brief, evidence, desired result, constraints and no-go conditions.

## 2. Scope and agree
Use a written proposal with deliverables, exclusions, dependencies, permissions, acceptance, price, payment stages, support and ownership. Customer approves customer-facing wording and consequential decisions. A paid assessment is optional, not required for every implementation.

Output: accepted scope and decision record. Unknown requirements stay explicit.

## 3. Payment and access
Verify payment status with the actual collection system; distinguish paid, pending and cleared. Obtain least necessary access through approved sharing methods. Keep credentials and client records in authorized systems. Do not put them in this knowledge repository.

Output: payment gate and access checklist. Never infer permission from possession of a session.

## 4. Implement
Prefer existing tools and deterministic steps where adequate. Eliminate unnecessary work before automating. Start in a safe preview, draft or dry run. Keep a recoverable prior state. Record important configuration and decisions.

Output: reviewable implementation linked to acceptance criteria.

## 5. Test
Test normal use, missing/invalid input, duplicates, failures, retries and escalation. For websites test desktop/mobile, contact paths, keyboard access, readable contrast, real approved content and deployment configuration. See QA checklist.

Output: dated pass/fail evidence and unresolved issues, not “looks good.”

## 6. Approval, balance and launch
Show the actual customer review version. Obtain acceptance or a specific correction list. Verify the agreed balance payment. Launch only to the explicitly authorized destination. Reopen the resulting live site/system and verify the saved/published version. A successful local build is not a live deployment.

Output: approval, payment state, exact destination/version and live verification.

## 7. Handoff
Provide ownership, operating steps, renewals/costs, account access instructions, support boundaries, recovery and known limitations. Do not retain an undocumented dependency on Donovin's personal account.

Output: handoff document and client acknowledgement.

## 8. Measure and close
Compare the agreed outcome with baseline over a suitable period. Record limitations and confounding factors. Ask permission before publishing a case study, testimonial, logo or identifiable example. Archive completed work separately from active obligations.

## Scope changes
Record requested change, reason, effect on cost/time/risk, and approval before implementation. Pause the affected work if requirements materially conflict. Do not silently absorb recurring support into a one-time fee.

## Website trial preflight
For the proposed $500 scope, confirm one page, one bounded revision, client-supplied/approved content, contact destination, domain/hosting ownership and third-party renewal cost. Agree materials/access deadlines and the effect of client delays. Use a reusable starter without generic/fabricated proof. Keep the lightest viable process; do not force a multi-week paid assessment or upsell into a simple website engagement. Balance is due after approval before authorized launch.

Keep a named recovery/rollback owner and a copy of the delivered version. No launch, handoff, settlement or result is inferred from a local build. Source A02/U02.

## Operating cadence and exception handling

Proposed practice: start each working day by checking cash obligations, active client blockers and next committed follow-ups. Keep one simple project list with owner, next action, date, current stage and dependency; hold new work when existing commitments exceed realistic delivery capacity. Each week reconcile settled payments and invoices, review project hours and commitments, follow up with permission, and document decisions. Store agreements, approved assets and acceptance records in a restricted client folder, with a link rather than credentials in the tracker.

At kickoff write the problem, baseline, decision-maker, scope, permissions, schedule dependencies, acceptance and escalation route. Send a concise status update when agreed: completed, next, client input needed and deadline impact. If client inputs are late, notify them and agree a new date; do not silently assume approval. Log requests as defect, in-scope revision or change; obtain approval on any extra price/time before starting the latter. If AI generates code or content, a human checks factual claims, security, licenses and functionality; use only permitted data.

Before acceptance, use the [working forms](../10-reference-and-archive/templates/website-project-forms.md) and [QA checklist](qa-launch-and-support.md). If a serious fault is found after launch, acknowledge, record impact and time, contain or roll back with appropriate authority, identify owner and next update, fix and verify, then record the lesson. Support commitments and fees come only from the signed agreement. After closing, reconcile cash, time, client approval, access transfer and open issues. Request a testimonial or case study only with permission; never invent one.

## Solo-business responsibility and control map

Donovin owns commercial decisions, delivery promises, client relationship, technical review and release decisions within the client's permission. The client owns its business facts, access authorization, named approver, content rights, acceptance and payment responsibilities. AI agents may prepare research, drafts, code, checklists and test evidence within authorization; they do not become contract signatories, approve client facts, spend money, contact customers or publish by inference. An agent reporting success is evidence to verify, not a substitute for acceptance.

Use one project record, one task list and a restricted client folder. Minimum folder contents: agreement/scope, brief, approved assets, access-owner list without secrets, decisions/change requests, QA evidence, acceptance/handoff and financial record. Avoid a new CRM until the existing tracker fails a specific need.

## Lifecycle gates and required records

| Stage | Required input and action | Output and gate | If blocked |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inquiry | Log source, date, request and contact permission | Next conversation or decline | Ask one needed clarification; do not build on guesswork |
| Qualification | Verify problem, buyer, fit and capacity | Pursue/hold/decline with reason | Hold unknown authority, access or economics |
| Discovery | Map real workflow and baseline | Client brief and success criterion | Use bounded measurement if cause is uncertain |
| Proposal | Estimate all work and dependencies | Written deliverables, exclusions, terms and acceptance | Resolve conflicting promises before agreement |
| Agreement | Named decision-maker accepts exact version | Dated acceptance and obligations | No presumed consent from silence |
| Payment | Issue agreed invoice and verify status | Receipt/settlement record and next gate | Follow agreed payment terms; pending is not available |
| Onboarding | Collect approved facts/assets and access invitations | Ready checklist and schedule | Notify missing inputs and agree revised dates |
| Implementation | Work privately against accepted scope | Reviewable draft and change log | Escalate feasibility or scope changes early |
| Testing | Run agreed normal, error and recovery cases | Pass/fail evidence and issue list | Fix release blockers and retest affected paths |
| Approval | Approver reviews exact artifact and exceptions | Written acceptance | Resolve defects; price additions separately |
| Handoff | Meet payment and launch gates, transfer ownership | Operating guide, access confirmation and live verification if authorized | Hold unauthorized release or failed transfer |
| Follow-up | Check agreed result and support boundary | Outcome, profitability and retrospective | Record limitations; never invent attribution |

## Onboarding and project planning procedure

At kickoff confirm scope version, approved contact, content owner, access approver, desired launch window, dependencies and escalation channel. Build tasks from acceptance criteria rather than a generic feature list. Give each task an owner, next action, due date and evidence of completion. Estimate time including client review and waiting separately from hands-on work. Use a proposed work-in-progress limit of one substantial build at a time until actual capacity supports more; Donovin may choose otherwise after reviewing obligations.

Send an agreed cadence update: completed work with preview/evidence, next task, input needed from whom by when, risk and date impact. If a customer does not respond, send one clear reminder through the agreed route and pause the dependent task; reset timing by agreement. Never convert silence into approval. For urgent defects, use the agreed incident route rather than waiting for the next routine update.

## Scope, pricing and collection controls

Keep an accepted baseline. A defect means the agreed behavior fails; an in-scope revision changes a detail within the agreed round; a change adds or alters scope. Record request, reason, acceptance effect, hours/cost, schedule and approval. If classification is disputed, show the baseline and agree a resolution before extra work. Preserve the working version while resolving it.

Invoice according to agreed milestones, with amount, reference, due date and payment route. Verify payment in the source account without storing bank details in the knowledge base. Log billed, received, settled and available amounts separately. On overdue payment, confirm receipt of the invoice, ask about the issue, and apply the actual agreement; do not invent late fees, seize access or disable a site. Refund/cancellation treatment depends on the accepted terms and applicable requirements; obtain professional advice where needed. Do not label a deposit nonrefundable by default.

## Access, data and software ownership

Inventory required systems and ask for the minimum role needed. Prefer invitations to client-owned accounts; record the owner, access purpose, scope and removal date. Use approved secret storage, never passwords in forms or source. Keep a recovery owner and verify the client can access the service. Before using an AI tool, identify what data leaves the system, applicable terms and client permission; use synthetic or redacted examples when possible. Do not copy resident, payment or private customer data into a general prompt or this public-capable archive.

For new software record account owner, billing owner, renewal, licenses, export/recovery route and dependencies. Confirm what happens if Donovin or a vendor is unavailable. Remove temporary access after acceptance where appropriate and record removal; preserve necessary support access only by agreement. Data retention and deletion require agreed policy and authorized action; do not improvise deletion of client records.

## Acceptance, troubleshooting and retrospective

Acceptance must identify the exact version, tested criteria, known exceptions, client approver and date. Handoff includes ordinary operating steps, update instructions, renewals, support contact and boundaries, backup/rollback location and remaining issues. Ask the client to demonstrate a routine task or confirm access; sending a login invitation alone does not prove successful transfer.

For a failure, record expected/actual behavior, reproducible steps, time, environment and impact. Stop risky repeated actions such as duplicate sends or charges. Contain using an approved reversible step, notify the responsible owner, inspect evidence, fix the cause and verify normal/error paths before restoring service. Record the next update time. A tool outage is a dependency, not proof the client caused the problem.

At close, compare estimated and actual hours by stage, collected revenue, direct costs, overhead allocation, support/rework and effective compensation. Ask what the client can now do and what remains difficult. Write one specific lesson with a process change and owner; update reusable instructions only when it generalizes. Measure the result over the agreed window and distinguish client reports from observed data. Seek testimonial/referral permission separately from project acceptance.
